# End-of-Day Review & Professional Routine

The trading session may end, but a professional trader's work is not finished.

One of the biggest differences between successful traders and struggling traders is what happens after the market closes.

Beginners often close their trading platform and move on.

Professionals review.

Improvement happens during review, not only during trading.

---

# Why End-of-Day Reviews Matter

Every trading day provides valuable information.

Whether the day was profitable or unprofitable, it contains lessons.

Reviewing each trading session helps traders:

- improve discipline
- identify repeated mistakes
- reinforce good habits
- strengthen emotional control
- improve future decision-making

A trader who never reviews is likely to repeat the same mistakes.

---

# Separate Emotion from Evaluation

Before reviewing the day, allow emotions to settle.

Do not evaluate yourself while feeling:

- angry
- frustrated
- excited
- disappointed
- overconfident

A calm review is usually more accurate than an emotional one.

---

# Complete Your Trading Journal

Every trade should be fully documented.

Record:

- market traded
- setup
- entry
- stop-loss
- take-profit
- position size
- trade result
- emotional state
- lessons learned

Honest records create meaningful improvement.

---

# Review Every Trade

Do not review only losing trades.

Review:

- winning trades
- losing trades
- break-even trades
- trades that were never entered
- markets you intentionally avoided

Every decision teaches something.

---

# Ask the Right Questions

For every trade ask:

- Did I follow my trading plan?
- Did I wait for confirmation?
- Was my entry disciplined?
- Was my stop-loss technically placed?
- Was my target realistic?
- Did I manage the trade correctly?
- Would I take the same trade again?

These questions improve consistency.

---

# Evaluate the Quality of Execution

A trade should not be judged only by profit.

Instead ask:

- Was the execution professional?
- Did I respect every rule?
- Did I remain patient?
- Did emotions influence my decisions?

Good execution should be recognised even when a trade loses.

---

# Review Your Risk Management

Review every day's risk.

Ask:

- Did I remain within my risk limits?
- Did I respect every stop-loss?
- Did I increase position size emotionally?
- Did I exceed my planned exposure?

For this course:

**Total trading session risk must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

Capital preservation always comes first.

---

# Evaluate Risk-to-Reward

Review whether your trades respected the trading plan.

For this course, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

Ask yourself:

- Were my targets realistic?
- Did I close winning trades too early?
- Did I allow losing trades to grow?

Reviewing these questions improves future trade management.

---

# Review Your Emotional Performance

Emotions influence trading more than many beginners realise.

Ask:

- Was I patient?
- Was I fearful?
- Was I greedy?
- Was I frustrated?
- Was I overconfident?
- Did emotions affect any decisions?

Recognising emotional patterns helps prevent repeated mistakes.

---

# Identify Your Best Decisions

Not every review should focus on mistakes.

Also identify what you did well.

Examples include:

- excellent patience
- disciplined execution
- proper position sizing
- following the checklist
- respecting stop-losses
- waiting for confirmation

Repeat successful habits.

---

# Identify Areas for Improvement

Every trader has something to improve.

Examples include:

- entering too early
- exiting too early
- moving stop-losses
- overtrading
- poor patience
- emotional decisions
- inconsistent journaling

Improvement begins with honest evaluation.

---

# Review Missed Opportunities

Sometimes the best lesson comes from trades you never entered.

Ask:

- Did I correctly avoid poor trades?
- Did I miss a valid setup?
- If I missed it, why?
- Was I distracted?
- Was I impatient?

Missing a trade is not always a mistake.

---

# Review Markets You Ignored

Markets you decided not to trade also deserve review.

Ask:

- Was avoiding them the correct decision?
- Did they fail to meet my rules?
- Would entering have violated my trading plan?

Professional traders are proud of good "no trade" decisions.

---

# Save and Review Chart Screenshots

Screenshots provide valuable visual records.

Save charts:

- before entry
- after entry
- during management
- after exit

Reviewing screenshots often reveals mistakes that numbers cannot show.

---

# Measure Your Discipline

Rate yourself honestly.

Possible categories:

- Excellent
- Good
- Fair
- Needs Improvement

Judge yourself on:

- following rules
- emotional control
- patience
- execution

Discipline is more important than today's profit.

---

# Plan Tomorrow's Improvement

Every review should end with one improvement goal.

Examples:

- Wait longer for confirmation.
- Complete the checklist before every trade.
- Reduce unnecessary chart watching.
- Improve journal quality.
- Stay within planned risk.

Small improvements produce long-term success.

---

# Develop a Professional Routine

Professional traders follow routines consistently.

A daily routine may include:

Morning

- Prepare for the market.

During Trading

- Follow the trading plan.

After Trading

- Complete the journal.
- Review every trade.
- Record lessons.
- Plan improvements.

Routine creates consistency.

---

# Protect Your Health

Trading performance depends on physical and mental health.

Maintain:

- quality sleep
- regular exercise
- healthy nutrition
- hydration
- stress management

A healthy trader usually makes better decisions.

---

# Continue Learning

Every trading day is part of your education.

Continue studying:

- market behaviour
- trading psychology
- risk management
- new market developments
- your own journal

Professional traders remain lifelong students.

---

# Accept Imperfection

No trader wins every trade.

No system is perfect.

The objective is continuous improvement rather than perfection.

Accepting this reduces unnecessary emotional pressure.

---

# Build Confidence Through Consistency

Confidence should come from:

- preparation
- discipline
- evidence
- repeated execution

It should never come from one profitable trade.

True confidence develops gradually.

---

# The Professional Trading Cycle

Every trading day should follow this cycle:

1. Prepare
2. Analyse
3. Plan
4. Execute
5. Manage
6. Review
7. Improve

Then repeat the process the next trading day.

This cycle creates continuous growth.

Today's Summary

Losing Trades: 1

Approximately 1:3

Session Risk:

Stayed below 5%

Strengths:

- Waited for confirmation.
- Followed the checklist.
- Controlled emotions.

Areas to Improve:

- Closed one winning trade too early.

Tomorrow's Goal:

Allow winning trades to reach planned targets when market conditions remain valid.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- ending the day without reviewing trades
- recording only profits
- ignoring emotional mistakes
- blaming the market
- hiding rule violations
- skipping the trading journal
- refusing to learn from losses
- failing to set improvement goals

Professional traders improve because they review honestly.

- identifying weaknesses
- setting improvement goals
- preparing for the next trading day
