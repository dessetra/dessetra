# Preparing Before the Market Opens

Professional trading begins before a trade is placed.

Many beginners open their charts and immediately start searching for entries.

Professional traders prepare first.

Preparation helps the trader understand:

- what is happening in the market
- which markets deserve attention
- where important price levels are located
- which economic events may affect volatility
- whether suitable trading opportunities are available

A well-prepared trader reacts according to a plan rather than emotion.

---

# Why Market Preparation Matters

The market can move quickly.

Without preparation, traders may:

- chase price
- enter late
- overlook important news
- trade against the larger trend
- take low-quality setups
- increase risk emotionally

Preparation reduces confusion.

It creates a clear plan before market pressure begins.

---

# Preparation Does Not Mean Prediction

The purpose of preparation is not to predict every market movement.

No trader knows exactly what the market will do.

Preparation means identifying possible scenarios.

For example:

- If price reaches support and confirms a reversal, look for a possible buy.
- If support breaks and price confirms below it, avoid buying.
- If major news is approaching, wait before entering.

Professional traders prepare for possibilities rather than depending on one prediction.

---

# Build a Consistent Pre-Market Routine

A professional routine should be simple and repeatable.

A basic pre-market routine may include:

1. Check personal readiness.
2. Review the economic calendar.
3. Review higher-timeframe market structure.
4. Mark important price levels.
5. Identify market conditions.
6. Create a watchlist.
7. Define possible trading scenarios.
8. Confirm risk limits.
9. Wait for valid setups.

Following the same process every trading day improves consistency.

---

# Check Your Personal Readiness

Before analyzing the market, evaluate yourself.

Ask:

- Am I calm?
- Am I focused?
- Am I tired?
- Am I angry or frustrated?
- Am I trying to recover a previous loss?
- Am I under pressure to make money today?

A trader who is emotionally or physically unprepared may make poor decisions.

Sometimes the most professional decision is not to trade.

---

# Review the Economic Calendar

Economic events can create significant volatility.

Examples include:

- interest rate decisions
- inflation reports
- employment reports
- central bank speeches
- gross domestic product reports
- major political or economic announcements

Before trading, identify:

- which events are scheduled
- the time of each event
- the currencies or markets affected
- the expected level of impact

Never enter a trade without knowing whether a major event is approaching.

---

# High-Impact News Events

High-impact events may cause:

- rapid price movement
- wider spreads
- slippage
- unpredictable volatility
- stop-loss activation

A trading system should define how news events are handled.

Possible rules include:

- no new trades shortly before major news
- close vulnerable trades before major announcements
- wait for volatility to settle
- trade only after confirmation

News risk should always be planned.

---

# Review Overnight Market Activity

Before the trading session begins, review what happened earlier.

Ask:

- Did price rise or fall significantly?
- Did the market break an important level?
- Was there unusual volatility?
- Did another session establish a clear trend?
- Is price currently consolidating?

This helps the trader understand the current market environment.

---

# Start with Higher Timeframes

Higher timeframes provide the broader market picture.

Examples include:

- Daily chart
- 4-hour chart
- 1-hour chart

Reviewing higher timeframes helps identify:

- major trend
- market structure
- strong support
- strong resistance
- major swing highs
- major swing lows
- consolidation zones

Lower-timeframe entries should be understood within the larger market context.

---

# Identify the Market Trend

Determine whether the market is:

- trending upward
- trending downward
- ranging
- transitioning between conditions

A market in an uptrend may show:

- higher highs
- higher lows

A market in a downtrend may show:

- lower highs
- lower lows

A ranging market may move repeatedly between support and resistance.

The trading plan should match the current market condition.

---

# Mark Important Support and Resistance

Support and resistance help identify areas where price may react.

Mark only meaningful levels.

Important levels may include:

- previous daily high
- previous daily low
- weekly high
- weekly low
- major swing highs
- major swing lows
- strong support zones
- strong resistance zones
- breakout levels

Avoid covering the chart with too many lines.

The objective is clarity.

---

# Identify Key Price Zones

Price does not always react at one exact number.

Sometimes it reacts within an area.

These areas may be called zones.

A zone may represent:

- support
- resistance
- supply
- demand
- consolidation
- previous breakout area

Zones should be based on visible price behaviour rather than guesswork.

---

# Check Current Volatility

Volatility measures how strongly price is moving.

Low volatility may produce:

- slow movement
- narrow ranges
- fewer opportunities

High volatility may produce:

- fast movement
- wider ranges
- increased risk
- larger price swings

The trader should adjust expectations according to current volatility.

---

# Review Market Sessions

Different trading sessions may behave differently.

Common sessions include:

- Asian Session
- London Session
- New York Session

Before trading, know:

- which session is active
- which session is about to open
- whether sessions overlap
- which markets are normally active during that period

Session awareness helps improve timing.

---

# Build a Daily Watchlist

A watchlist is a small group of markets selected for possible trading.

Examples may include:

- two or three Forex pairs
- one or two cryptocurrencies
- one index
- one commodity

A focused watchlist reduces distraction.

Trying to monitor too many markets can lead to rushed decisions.

---

# How to Select Watchlist Markets

A market may be added to the watchlist when:

- the trend is clear
- price is near an important level
- a valid setup may develop
- volatility is suitable
- the market matches the trading system

Markets should not be selected only because they are moving quickly.

---

# Remove Weak Opportunities

Not every market deserves attention.

Remove markets that show:

- unclear structure
- random movement
- excessive volatility
- very low volatility
- poor risk-to-reward
- no valid setup
- major news risk

Filtering weak markets protects the trader from unnecessary trades.

---

# Create Trading Scenarios

A trading scenario explains what the trader will do if a specific condition occurs.

Example:

## Bullish Scenario

- Price reaches support.
- Bullish confirmation appears.
- Market structure remains positive.
- A buy setup may be considered.

## Bearish Scenario

- Support breaks.
- Price confirms below the level.
- A sell setup may be considered after a valid retest.

## No-Trade Scenario

- Price remains between important levels.
- No confirmation appears.
- The trader waits.

Scenarios help reduce impulsive decisions.

---

# Define Entry Areas in Advance

Before the market becomes active, identify areas where a trade may become possible.

These areas may include:

- support
- resistance
- breakout zones
- pullback zones
- trend continuation areas

The trader should not enter simply because price reaches an area.

Confirmation is still required.

---

# Plan Stop-Loss Locations

Stop-loss planning should happen before entry.

Possible stop-loss locations may be based on:

- market structure
- technical invalidation
- swing highs
- swing lows
- support and resistance
- volatility

A stop-loss should never be placed randomly.

---

# Plan Potential Targets

Potential targets should also be identified before entry.

Targets may be based on:

- previous highs
- previous lows
- support
- resistance
- measured price movement
- fixed risk-to-reward

For this course, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

This means risking one unit to pursue approximately three units of potential reward when market conditions support it.

---

# Confirm Risk Before Trading

Before the session begins, define:

- maximum risk per trade
- maximum number of trades
- maximum session loss
- total session risk

For this course:

**Total risk during one trading session must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

This limit should be decided before emotions become involved.

---

# Prepare the Trading Platform

Before trading, confirm that:

- the internet connection is stable
- the trading platform is working
- charts are loading properly
- account balance is correct
- position-size calculations are available
- stop-loss and take-profit tools are functioning

Technical preparation prevents avoidable mistakes.

---

# Prepare the Trading Journal

Open the trading journal before the session begins.

Prepare to record:

- market
- setup
- entry
- stop-loss
- target
- risk
- emotional state
- result
- lesson learned

A journal should be part of the trading process, not an afterthought.

---

# Create a Pre-Trade Checklist

Before considering any trade, ask:

1. Is the market on my watchlist?
2. Is the higher-timeframe trend clear?
3. Is price near an important level?
4. Is there a valid setup?
5. Has confirmation appeared?
6. Is major news approaching?
7. Is the stop-loss clear?
8. Is the target realistic?
9. Does the trade support approximately 1:3 risk-to-reward?
10. Will total session risk remain below 5%?
11. Am I emotionally prepared?

If important conditions are missing, do not trade.

---

# Prepare for No Trade

A professional trader does not force opportunities.

Some days may produce:

- no valid setup
- unclear market structure
- poor risk-to-reward
- excessive volatility
- low volatility
- major news uncertainty

A no-trade day can still be a successful day.

Capital protected is capital available for future opportunities.

---

# Avoid Overanalysis

Preparation should create clarity.

It should not create confusion.

Avoid:

- using too many indicators
- marking too many levels
- analyzing too many markets
- creating too many possible scenarios
- changing the plan repeatedly

A simple plan is easier to follow.

---

# Example Pre-Market Routine

A trader may follow this routine:

## Step 1: Personal Check

Confirm mental and physical readiness.

## Step 2: News Review

Check scheduled economic events.

## Step 3: Higher-Timeframe Analysis

Review the Daily and 4-hour charts.

## Step 4: Mark Levels

Identify important support and resistance.

## Step 5: Build Watchlist

Select only the best markets.

## Step 6: Create Scenarios

Prepare bullish, bearish and no-trade possibilities.

## Step 7: Confirm Risk

Set maximum trade and session risk.

## Step 8: Wait

Do not enter until the setup and confirmation appear.

This routine can be adjusted to suit the trader's system.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- opening charts and trading immediately
- ignoring economic news
- trading without reviewing higher timeframes
- watching too many markets
- forcing setups
- failing to prepare stop-losses
- failing to define targets
- trading while emotional
- exceeding session risk
- entering before confirmation

Preparation helps prevent these mistakes.

---

# Key Principle

Professional traders prepare before they participate.

They do not begin the day by asking:

"How much money can I make?"

They begin by asking:

"What is the market showing, what does my system allow and how will I protect my capital?"

Preparation creates discipline before pressure begins.

---

# Lesson Summary

Before the market opens, a professional trader should:

- evaluate personal readiness
- review the economic calendar
- study higher-timeframe structure
- identify the trend
- mark important levels
- review volatility
- build a focused watchlist
- create trading scenarios
- plan entries, stop-losses and targets
- confirm session risk
- prepare the journal
- wait for valid confirmation

For this course:

- traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately **1:3**
- total trading session risk must **never exceed 5%** of total trading capital

The objective of preparation is not to predict the market.

It is to be ready for whatever the market presents.
