# Drawdown Control & Capital Preservation

Every trader experiences periods when the account balance declines.

This decline is called drawdown.

Drawdown is normal in trading.

The danger is not simply experiencing a loss.

The danger is allowing a manageable loss to grow into a severe account decline.

Professional traders do not judge themselves only by how much profit they make.

They also monitor:

- how much capital they lose
- how quickly losses increase
- how long recovery takes
- whether risk limits were respected
- whether the trading strategy is still performing as expected

Capital preservation is the foundation of long-term survival.

---

# What Is Drawdown?

Drawdown is the reduction in account value from a previous peak to a later low.

It measures how much the account has declined before recovering or reaching a new peak.

Example:

Account peak:

$10,000

Account falls to:

$8,500

Drawdown:

$1,500

Percentage drawdown:

15%

---

# Drawdown Formula

Use this formula:

**Drawdown Percentage = (Peak Balance - Current Balance) ÷ Peak Balance × 100**

Example:

Peak balance:

$5,000

Current balance:

$4,250

Difference:

$750

Calculation:

$750 ÷ $5,000 × 100 = 15%

The account is in a 15% drawdown.

---

# Balance Drawdown vs Equity Drawdown

Account balance reflects completed trades.

Account equity includes:

- account balance
- unrealized profit
- unrealized loss

A trader may appear profitable based on closed trades while open positions are carrying large losses.

This creates equity drawdown.

Professional traders monitor both balance and equity.

---

# Maximum Drawdown

Maximum drawdown is the largest decline from a previous account peak to the lowest point before a new peak is reached.

It helps evaluate:

- strategy risk
- account stability
- emotional pressure
- capital protection
- recovery difficulty

A strategy may be profitable overall but still be unsuitable if its maximum drawdown is too large.

---

# Why Drawdown Matters

Drawdown affects more than account balance.

It can influence:

- confidence
- decision-making
- position sizing
- emotional discipline
- strategy trust
- recovery time

Large drawdowns often lead traders to abandon their plans or take excessive risks.

Controlling drawdown protects both capital and psychology.

---

# The Mathematics of Recovery

The larger the loss, the larger the percentage gain required to recover.

Consider these examples:

| Account Loss | Gain Required to Recover |
|---|---:|
| 5% | Approximately 5.3% |
| 10% | Approximately 11.1% |
| 20% | 25% |
| 30% | Approximately 42.9% |
| 40% | Approximately 66.7% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 60% | 150% |
| 75% | 300% |
| 90% | 900% |

This shows why preventing large losses is more important than chasing rapid profits.

---

# Recovery Example

Starting capital:

$1,000

Loss:

20%

Remaining balance:

$800

To return from $800 to $1,000, the trader needs:

$200 profit

$200 is 25% of $800.

Therefore, a 20% loss requires a 25% gain to recover.

---

# Why Small Losses Are Easier to Recover

Small losses preserve most of the account.

Example:

Account balance:

$2,000

Loss:

5%

Remaining balance:

$1,900

Required recovery:

Approximately 5.3%

This is much easier than recovering from a 50% loss.

Professional traders therefore focus on keeping drawdowns shallow.

---

# Capital Preservation

Capital preservation means protecting the account from severe losses so that the trader can continue participating in future opportunities.

It involves:

- controlling risk per trade
- limiting session losses
- avoiding excessive leverage
- reducing overexposure
- respecting stop-losses
- avoiding emotional decisions
- pausing when performance declines
- reviewing the trading plan

Capital preservation does not mean avoiding all losses.

It means ensuring losses remain controlled.

---

# Survival Before Growth

Many beginners focus on growing the account quickly.

Professional traders first focus on survival.

The correct order is:

1. Protect capital.
2. Build consistency.
3. Improve execution.
4. Grow gradually.

An account that survives can recover.

An account that is destroyed cannot benefit from future opportunities.

---

# Risk Per Trade and Drawdown

Risk per trade directly affects how quickly drawdown can grow.

Consider ten consecutive losses.

### Trader A

Risk per trade:

1%

Approximate total decline:

Less than 10% because the risk amount reduces slightly as the balance falls.

### Trader B

Risk per trade:

5%

Approximate total decline:

About 40% after compounding losses.

This difference can determine whether the trader remains disciplined or becomes emotionally unstable.

---

# Maximum Session Risk

For this course:

**A trader should never risk above 5% of total trading capital in one trading session.**

This is a maximum ceiling, not a recommended target.

Many disciplined traders may use a much lower limit.

Once the session loss limit is reached, trading should stop.

---

# Why Trading Must Stop at the Session Limit

Continuing after the maximum session loss can lead to:

- revenge trading
- overtrading
- poor-quality setups
- larger position sizes
- emotional decisions
- rapid account decline

Stopping protects the trader from making decisions under stress.

The market will provide more opportunities later.

---

# Daily, Weekly and Monthly Loss Limits

A complete risk plan may define:

- maximum loss per trade
- maximum loss per session
- maximum daily loss
- maximum weekly loss
- maximum monthly drawdown

Example:

- Per trade: 1%
- Per session: 3%
- Per week: 6%
- Per month: 10%

The exact limits depend on the strategy and trader.

However, every limit should be defined before trading begins.

---

# Drawdown Thresholds

A trader may create different response levels.

Example:

### Mild Drawdown

0% to 5%

Action:

- continue following the plan
- monitor execution
- avoid emotional changes

### Moderate Drawdown

5% to 10%

Action:

- review recent trades
- reduce unnecessary exposure
- confirm that rules are being followed

### Serious Drawdown

10% to 15%

Action:

- reduce position size
- stop trading weak setups
- investigate strategy performance
- consider taking a temporary pause

### Severe Drawdown

Above 15%

Action:

- stop live trading
- conduct a complete review
- return to simulation or demo practice
- resume only after identifying the cause

These percentages are examples and should be adapted to the trader's plan.

---

# Reducing Position Size During Drawdown

When an account enters drawdown, the trader may reduce risk.

Example:

Normal risk per trade:

1%

Reduced risk during drawdown:

0.5%

This helps:

- slow further decline
- reduce emotional pressure
- protect remaining capital
- allow careful recovery

Increasing risk during drawdown usually creates greater danger.

---

# Never Chase Recovery

After a loss, beginners often attempt to recover immediately.

They may:

- double position size
- take more trades
- ignore confirmation
- trade unfamiliar markets
- remove stop-losses

This behaviour is called chasing recovery.

Recovery should come from disciplined execution, not desperation.

---

# Revenge Trading

Revenge trading occurs when a trader enters new positions emotionally after a loss.

The objective is usually to win back money quickly.

Signs of revenge trading include:

- entering immediately after being stopped out
- increasing position size
- ignoring the strategy
- taking low-quality setups
- trading outside normal hours
- feeling angry at the market

The correct response after a difficult loss may be to stop and review.

---

# Loss Aversion

Loss aversion is the tendency to feel the pain of a loss more strongly than the satisfaction of an equal gain.

It may cause traders to:

- hold losing positions too long
- close winning positions too early
- move stop-losses
- avoid valid setups after a loss
- take excessive risk to recover

A written plan helps reduce the effect of loss aversion.

---

# Recency Bias

Recency bias occurs when a trader gives too much importance to recent outcomes.

Examples:

- believing the strategy is perfect after three wins
- believing the strategy is useless after three losses
- increasing size after a short winning streak
- abandoning the plan after a short losing streak

A strategy should be evaluated over a meaningful sample of trades.

---

# Losing Streaks

A losing streak is a sequence of consecutive losing trades.

Even profitable strategies can experience losing streaks.

For example, a strategy with a 60% win rate can still produce several losses in a row.

Traders must be financially and emotionally prepared for this possibility.

---

# Winning Streaks Can Also Create Risk

Winning streaks can produce:

- overconfidence
- larger position sizes
- lower-quality entries
- rule-breaking
- excessive leverage

The trader may believe recent success proves that future trades will also win.

Discipline must remain unchanged during both winning and losing periods.

---

# Strategy Drawdown vs Execution Drawdown

Not every drawdown has the same cause.

### Strategy Drawdown

Occurs when the strategy experiences a normal period of poor performance.

The trader follows all rules, but market conditions are less favourable.

### Execution Drawdown

Occurs because the trader violates the plan.

Examples include:

- entering early
- overtrading
- risking too much
- ignoring stop-losses
- trading emotionally

The solution depends on the cause.

---

# Market-Condition Drawdown

A strategy may perform well in one market condition and poorly in another.

Examples:

- a trend-following strategy may struggle in sideways markets
- a range strategy may struggle during strong breakouts
- a low-volatility strategy may struggle during major news

A trader should understand which conditions support the strategy.

---

# Reviewing Drawdown

When drawdown increases, review:

- Was every trade valid?
- Was risk calculated correctly?
- Were stop-losses respected?
- Were positions correlated?
- Was leverage excessive?
- Was important news ignored?
- Did market conditions change?
- Was the strategy followed consistently?
- Was the sample size large enough?
- Were trading costs included?

The review should be based on evidence, not emotion.

---

# Trading Journal

A trading journal helps identify the causes of drawdown.

Record:

- date and time
- market traded
- entry price
- stop-loss
- take-profit
- position size
- risk percentage
- trade result
- strategy used
- market condition
- emotional state
- lesson learned

Without records, the trader may repeat the same mistakes.

---

# Useful Drawdown Statistics

A trader may monitor:

- current drawdown
- maximum drawdown
- average drawdown
- longest losing streak
- average losing trade
- largest losing trade
- recovery time
- number of rule violations

These statistics reveal more than profit alone.

---

# The Equity Curve

An equity curve is a chart showing how account value changes over time.

A healthy equity curve may rise gradually with controlled declines.

A dangerous equity curve may show:

- sharp gains
- sudden deep losses
- unstable performance
- repeated large drawdowns

The objective is not a perfectly straight line.

The objective is stable, controlled growth.

---

# Capital Allocation

Capital allocation determines how much money is assigned to:

- trading
- reserve funds
- different strategies
- different markets

A trader should not expose all available capital at once.

Keeping reserve capital may reduce pressure and improve flexibility.

---

# Reserve Capital

Reserve capital is money kept outside active trades.

It may help cover:

- future opportunities
- emergency withdrawals
- strategy adjustments
- temporary losses

Reserve capital should not be used emotionally to rescue uncontrolled losing positions.

---

# Avoiding Full Account Exposure

Using the entire account as margin or collateral creates serious risk.

A sudden move may cause:

- liquidation
- margin call
- large slippage
- inability to manage the position

Only a controlled portion of capital should be exposed.

---

# Leverage and Drawdown

Leverage accelerates both profit and loss.

A small adverse price movement can create a large percentage loss when leverage is excessive.

The higher the leverage, the less room the trade has to survive normal market movement.

Leverage should be used carefully and only within the risk plan.

---

# Compounding Losses

Losses also compound.

Example:

A trader loses 10% of the account.

Then loses another 10% of the remaining balance.

The total decline is not exactly 20%.

Starting balance:

$1,000

After first 10% loss:

$900

After second 10% loss:

$810

Total loss:

$190 or 19%

Repeated percentage losses steadily reduce capital.

---

# Compounding Profits Responsibly

Profits may increase future position size when percentage risk remains constant.

However, traders should not increase risk simply because the account has recently grown.

Position size should be recalculated from the updated balance while respecting the same risk rules.

---

# Withdrawals and Capital Protection

Some traders periodically withdraw part of their profits.

This may help:

- protect realized gains
- reduce the amount exposed
- separate trading capital from personal funds

Withdrawals should follow a planned schedule.

They should not leave the account undercapitalized.

---

# Recovery Plan

A responsible recovery plan may include:

1. Stop trading after the maximum loss limit.
2. Review every recent trade.
3. Separate strategy losses from execution mistakes.
4. Reduce position size.
5. Return to high-quality setups only.
6. Avoid increasing leverage.
7. Continue journaling.
8. Resume normal risk only after consistent execution improves.

Recovery should be gradual.

---

# Example Recovery Process

Account peak:

$5,000

Current balance:

$4,500

Drawdown:

10%

Normal risk per trade:

1%

Temporary recovery risk:

0.5%

The trader focuses on:

- fewer trades
- higher-quality setups
- strict stop-losses
- no revenge trading
- full journal review

The goal is not to recover immediately.

The goal is to restore disciplined execution.

---

# When to Pause Live Trading

A trader should consider pausing when:

- session loss limit is reached
- several rules have been broken
- emotional control is weak
- the account reaches a serious drawdown threshold
- strategy performance changes significantly
- personal stress affects decision-making
- trading begins to feel compulsive

A temporary pause is a form of discipline.

It is not failure.

---

# Returning to Demo Trading

Demo trading can help during recovery.

It allows the trader to:

- test whether the strategy still works
- rebuild disciplined execution
- practise without financial pressure
- review new market conditions
- confirm revised rules

However, demo performance should not be used as an excuse to return to excessive live risk.

---

# Protecting Profits

Capital preservation also includes protecting accumulated profits.

A trader may:

- reduce risk after unusually strong growth
- withdraw part of realized profits
- avoid increasing leverage
- continue using the same risk percentage
- stop after reaching a planned session objective

Profits should not create careless behaviour.

---

# Profit Targets and Capital Preservation

A weekly or monthly target may provide structure.

However, a trader should not force trades simply to reach the target.

The market does not guarantee a fixed return every day or week.

Capital protection must remain more important than meeting an income target.

---

# The Danger of Guaranteed Return Thinking

No trader can guarantee a consistent percentage return without risk.

Statements such as guaranteed weekly profit can create unrealistic expectations.

Actual results may vary because of:

- market conditions
- strategy performance
- execution
- volatility
- costs
- losing streaks

Professional traders work with probabilities, not guarantees.

---

# Risk Adjustment Rules

A trader may define rules such as:

- reduce risk after a 5% drawdown
- pause after a 10% drawdown
- stop for the day after three losses
- stop for the week after reaching the weekly loss limit
- return to normal risk only after a defined review period

The rules must be written before drawdown occurs.

---

# Capital Preservation Checklist

Before each trading session, confirm:

- My account balance is known.
- My risk per trade is defined.
- My maximum session loss is defined.
- Total open exposure is controlled.
- Correlated trades are considered.
- Leverage is reasonable.
- Major news has been checked.
- My emotional state is stable.
- I know when I must stop trading.
- I am using non-essential capital.

---

# Drawdown Response Checklist

When drawdown occurs:

1. Stop emotional trading.
2. Calculate the actual drawdown.
3. Review the trading journal.
4. Identify rule violations.
5. Check whether market conditions changed.
6. Reduce risk if necessary.
7. Pause live trading if limits are exceeded.
8. Avoid chasing recovery.
9. Return only with a clear plan.
10. Focus on execution before profit.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes:

- ignoring account drawdown
- focusing only on profit
- increasing risk after losses
- attempting immediate recovery
- using excessive leverage
- trading after reaching the session loss limit
- refusing to accept a controlled loss
- depositing more money to support an uncontrolled position
- abandoning the strategy after a few losses
- trusting the strategy blindly without review
- using essential household money
- hiding losses from the trading journal
- forcing weekly or monthly profit targets

---

# Key Principle

The best traders are not those who avoid every loss.

They are those who prevent ordinary losses from becoming destructive.

Capital preservation gives the trader time, stability and future opportunities.

---

# Lesson Summary

Drawdown is the decline in account value from a previous peak.

It should be measured using both balance and equity.

Large drawdowns are difficult to recover because the percentage gain required increases as losses become deeper.

Professional drawdown control includes:

- limiting risk per trade
- respecting session, weekly and monthly loss limits
- reducing size during difficult periods
- avoiding revenge trading
- monitoring leverage and correlation
- reviewing journal data
- pausing when necessary
- recovering gradually

For this course, total risk during one trading session must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.

This is a maximum limit, not a target.

Capital preservation comes before profit because a protected account can continue participating in future market opportunities.
