# Fear, Greed, FOMO & Revenge Trading

The market does not force traders to make emotional decisions.

Instead, emotional decisions usually happen because traders react to uncertainty without following their trading plan.

Among the strongest emotional influences are:

- Fear
- Greed
- Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)
- Revenge Trading

Nearly every trader experiences these emotions.

Professional traders are not successful because they never experience them.

They are successful because they have learned how to manage them.

---

# Why These Four Emotions Matter

These emotions affect almost every trading decision.

They influence:

- trade selection
- position size
- stop-loss placement
- take-profit decisions
- trade management
- confidence
- discipline
- long-term consistency

When uncontrolled, these emotions can destroy months of disciplined trading within a few hours.

---

# Understanding Fear

Fear is the emotional response to the possibility of loss.

In trading, fear often appears before, during or after entering a trade.

Fear itself is not the problem.

It becomes dangerous when it changes the trader's behaviour.

---

# Common Causes of Fear

Fear may develop because of:

- risking too much money
- recent losing trades
- lack of confidence
- lack of experience
- financial pressure
- trading with borrowed money
- unclear strategy
- uncertainty
- negative past experiences

Identifying the source of fear helps determine the appropriate solution.

---

# Fear Before Entering

Before entry, fear may cause a trader to:

- ignore valid setups
- hesitate
- wait too long
- miss opportunities
- continuously seek more confirmation
- doubt the trading plan

The market may move without the trader.

This often creates regret.

---

# Fear During a Trade

While a trade is open, fear may cause the trader to:

- close profits too early
- move the stop-loss closer
- reduce position size emotionally
- watch every price movement
- panic during normal pullbacks
- abandon the original plan

Short-term price movement often creates emotional pressure.

---

# Fear After a Loss

Following a losing trade, fear may cause:

- avoiding the next valid setup
- reducing confidence
- changing strategy too quickly
- believing the strategy no longer works
- refusing to trade

One controlled loss should never determine future decisions.

---

# Healthy Fear vs Unhealthy Fear

Healthy fear encourages:

- risk calculation
- careful planning
- proper preparation
- respect for uncertainty

Unhealthy fear causes:

- paralysis
- hesitation
- emotional exits
- rule breaking
- missed opportunities

The objective is not to eliminate fear.

The objective is to prevent fear from controlling decisions.

---

# Reducing Fear

Fear can often be reduced by:

- using smaller position sizes
- testing the strategy
- keeping realistic expectations
- accepting uncertainty
- following the trading plan
- using proper stop-losses
- limiting total session risk

Confidence grows through preparation rather than prediction.

---

# Understanding Greed

Greed is the desire to earn more than the trading plan allows.

Greed often appears after profits or during fast-moving markets.

Instead of following the plan, the trader begins seeking additional profit regardless of increased risk.

---

# Signs of Greed

Possible signs include:

- increasing position size randomly
- removing take-profit targets
- entering several correlated trades
- using excessive leverage
- refusing to close profitable trades
- opening trades without confirmation
- ignoring session limits

Greed usually encourages the trader to believe that "one more trade" will produce even greater profit.

---

# Why Greed Is Dangerous

Greed often leads to:

- excessive exposure
- poor risk-to-reward
- emotional decision-making
- larger drawdowns
- violation of trading rules

Many accounts are damaged not after losing streaks, but after profitable periods followed by overconfidence and greed.

---

# Controlling Greed

A trader can reduce greed by:

- keeping the original target
- respecting session limits
- avoiding unnecessary leverage
- using predefined position sizing
- following the trading journal
- accepting that no market opportunity lasts forever

Discipline should remain unchanged during winning periods.

---

# Understanding Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)

Fear of Missing Out (FOMO) occurs when the trader feels compelled to enter because price is already moving.

Instead of following the strategy, the trader fears being left behind.

FOMO often appears during:

- strong trends
- major breakouts
- viral social media discussions
- sudden news events
- rapid cryptocurrency rallies

---

# Signs of FOMO

A trader experiencing FOMO may:

- chase price
- ignore confirmation
- enter after large candles
- abandon risk calculations
- ignore poor risk-to-reward
- buy because everyone else is buying
- sell because everyone else is selling

The decision becomes emotional rather than analytical.

---

# The Cost of Chasing

Chasing price often produces:

- late entries
- wider stop-losses
- reduced reward potential
- poor execution
- emotional stress

By the time many traders enter emotionally, early participants may already be taking profit.

---

# Recognising FOMO

Ask yourself:

- Would I enter this trade if nobody else was talking about it?
- Does this setup match my written strategy?
- Am I entering because price is moving quickly?
- Has my original entry already passed?
- Is my risk-to-reward still acceptable?

If the answer is no, the trade should usually be avoided.

---

# Preventing FOMO

Practical methods include:

- accepting missed trades
- waiting for confirmation
- using alerts instead of watching every candle
- limiting social media influence
- following only written trading rules
- remembering that opportunities constantly return

Missing one trade is better than forcing a poor trade.

---

# Understanding Revenge Trading

Revenge trading occurs when the trader attempts to recover losses emotionally rather than logically.

The objective changes from following the strategy to recovering money as quickly as possible.

This is one of the most destructive behaviours in trading.

---

# Why Revenge Trading Happens

Common causes include:

- frustration
- anger
- embarrassment
- disappointment
- damaged confidence
- financial pressure
- refusing to accept a loss

Instead of accepting uncertainty, the trader attempts to defeat the market.

---

# Warning Signs of Revenge Trading

Possible warning signs include:

- immediately opening another trade
- increasing position size
- removing the stop-loss
- trading unfamiliar markets
- abandoning confirmation rules
- ignoring the session limit
- emotional urgency

The trader no longer behaves according to the trading plan.

---

# The Revenge Trading Cycle

A common cycle is:

1. Controlled loss.
2. Emotional frustration.
3. Immediate emotional trade.
4. Larger loss.
5. Greater frustration.
6. Even larger emotional trade.
7. Significant account damage.

Breaking this cycle requires stopping before step three.

---

# How to Prevent Revenge Trading

Possible rules include:

- stop after three consecutive losses
- leave the screen after every stopped-out trade
- record emotions before entering again
- reduce position size after difficult sessions
- never increase risk to recover losses
- review the journal before opening another position

Recovery should come from discipline, not emotion.

---

# Emotional Recovery After a Loss

After a losing trade:

1. Accept the result.
2. Confirm whether the rules were followed.
3. Record the trade.
4. Take a short break.
5. Return only if another valid setup appears.
6. Accept that no trade is also a valid decision.

A controlled loss is part of professional trading.

---

# Combining Fear and Greed

Fear and greed often alternate.

Example:

A trader loses money and becomes fearful.

The next trade wins.

Confidence quickly becomes greed.

The trader increases position size.

The following trade loses heavily.

Emotional extremes often replace consistent decision-making.

---

# Emotional Balance

The goal is emotional balance.

A balanced trader:

- accepts uncertainty
- respects risk
- follows the plan
- avoids emotional extremes
- judges performance over many trades

Balance produces consistency.

---

# The Role of Position Size

Position size strongly affects emotional behaviour.

When risk is too high:

- fear increases
- greed becomes stronger
- revenge trading becomes more likely
- FOMO becomes harder to resist

Smaller, controlled risk allows clearer thinking.

---

# Risk Management Supports Psychology

Psychology and risk management work together.

Good risk management reduces emotional pressure.

For this course:

- Traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately **1:3**, where realistic.
- Total risk during one trading session must **never exceed 5%** of total trading capital.

These limits help reduce emotional decision-making.

---

# Practical Emotional Rules

A trader may create rules such as:

- Never chase a missed trade.
- Never increase risk after a loss.
- Never increase leverage because of excitement.
- Always complete the pre-trade checklist.
- Stop trading when emotional control begins to weaken.
- Record every emotional mistake.

These rules should become part of the written trading plan.

---

# Emotional Pause Technique

Before entering an emotional trade:

1. Remove your hand from the trading controls.
2. Take several slow breaths.
3. Read your trading rules.
4. Check the setup again.
5. Calculate risk.
6. Decide only after completing the checklist.

A pause often prevents expensive mistakes.

---

# Learning From Emotional Mistakes

Every emotional mistake should become a lesson.

Ask:

- What emotion influenced me?
- What triggered it?
- Which rule did I ignore?
- How can I prevent this next time?

Mistakes become valuable only when they improve future behaviour.

---

# Building Emotional Discipline

Emotional discipline grows through:

- repetition
- journaling
- controlled risk
- patience
- experience
- honesty
- regular review

It cannot be developed overnight.

Like technical analysis, psychological discipline improves with deliberate practice.

---

# Key Principle

The market will always create opportunities.

A trader who protects emotional control can continue trading tomorrow.

A trader who allows fear, greed, FOMO or revenge trading to control decisions may lose both capital and confidence.

---

# Lesson Summary

Fear encourages hesitation and premature exits.

Greed encourages excessive risk and poor discipline.

Fear of Missing Out (FOMO) encourages chasing price instead of waiting for valid setups.

Revenge trading attempts to recover losses emotionally rather than logically.

Professional traders manage these emotions by:

- following written rules
- respecting risk limits
- accepting uncertainty
- journaling emotional behaviour
- keeping position sizes appropriate
- avoiding impulsive decisions

For this course:

- Traders should generally aim for an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately **1:3**, when market structure realistically supports it.
- Total risk during one trading session must **never exceed 5%** of trading capital.

Emotional control is not about eliminating emotions.

It is about making disciplined decisions even when emotions are present.
