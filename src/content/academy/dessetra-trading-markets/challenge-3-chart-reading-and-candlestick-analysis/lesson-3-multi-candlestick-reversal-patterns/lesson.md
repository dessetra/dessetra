# Multi-Candlestick Reversal Patterns

Multi-candlestick patterns are formations created by two or more candles.

They provide more information than a single candle because they show how control shifts between buyers and sellers over several periods.

These patterns may help traders identify:

- weakening momentum
- failed continuation
- rejection
- possible trend reversal
- market hesitation
- changes in buyer or seller control

However, no candlestick pattern guarantees a reversal.

A pattern becomes more meaningful when it appears:

- near support or resistance
- after an extended trend
- at a major higher-timeframe level
- after a false breakout or breakdown
- with confirmation
- in agreement with market structure

The goal is not to memorize names alone.

The goal is to understand how market control changes from one candle to the next.

---

# What Is a Reversal Pattern?

A reversal pattern is a candle formation that may indicate that the current market direction is weakening and could change.

A bullish reversal pattern may appear after a downward move.

A bearish reversal pattern may appear after an upward move.

A pattern should not be called a reversal simply because it looks familiar.

A genuine reversal normally requires:

- an existing trend or directional move
- weakening momentum
- a clear pattern
- confirmation
- a market structure change

Without an existing move, the pattern may only represent temporary indecision.

---

# Bullish Engulfing Pattern

A Bullish Engulfing pattern is formed by two candles.

The first candle is bearish.

The second candle is bullish and its body fully covers or engulfs the body of the first candle.

This pattern may show that buyers have taken control from sellers.

It becomes more meaningful when it appears:

- after a downtrend
- near support
- at a demand zone
- after a false breakdown
- at a higher-timeframe level

---

# Bullish Engulfing Psychology

The pattern tells this market story:

1. Sellers control the first candle.
2. The next candle may open lower or near the previous close.
3. Buyers enter aggressively.
4. Buyers push price above the previous candle's opening price.
5. The bullish candle closes strongly.
6. Control appears to shift from sellers to buyers.

The stronger the second candle closes, the stronger the rejection may appear.

Confirmation is still necessary.

---

# Bullish Engulfing Confirmation

Possible confirmation includes:

- the next candle closing higher
- price breaking above the engulfing candle's high
- support remaining intact
- a higher low forming
- increased buying volume
- a bullish market structure shift

A trader should avoid entering only because the pattern is present.

---

# Bearish Engulfing Pattern

A Bearish Engulfing pattern is formed by two candles.

The first candle is bullish.

The second candle is bearish and its body fully covers the body of the first candle.

It may show that sellers have taken control from buyers.

It becomes more meaningful when it appears:

- after an uptrend
- near resistance
- at a supply zone
- after a failed breakout
- at a major higher-timeframe level

---

# Bearish Engulfing Psychology

The pattern tells this story:

1. Buyers control the first candle.
2. The next candle may open higher or near the previous close.
3. Sellers enter aggressively.
4. Sellers push price below the previous candle's opening price.
5. The bearish candle closes strongly.
6. Control appears to shift from buyers to sellers.

A strong close near the low may indicate stronger selling pressure.

---

# Piercing Line Pattern

The Piercing Line is a two-candle bullish reversal pattern.

It normally appears after a downward move.

The first candle is bearish.

The second candle opens lower but closes well inside the body of the first candle.

Ideally, it closes above the midpoint of the first candle.

This may indicate that buyers are recovering control.

---

# Piercing Line Psychology

The market first appears strongly bearish.

The second candle may initially continue lower.

Buyers then enter and push price upward.

The higher close shows that sellers were unable to maintain control.

The pattern becomes more significant near support.

---

# Dark Cloud Cover

Dark Cloud Cover is a two-candle bearish reversal pattern.

It normally appears after an upward move.

The first candle is bullish.

The second candle opens higher but closes deeply inside the body of the first candle.

Ideally, it closes below the midpoint of the first candle.

This may indicate that sellers are beginning to take control.

---

# Dark Cloud Cover Psychology

The market first appears strongly bullish.

The second candle may initially move higher.

Sellers then enter and force price downward.

The lower close shows that buyers were unable to maintain control.

The pattern becomes more meaningful near resistance.

---

# Morning Star

The Morning Star is a three-candle bullish reversal pattern.

It normally appears after a downtrend.

It consists of:

1. A strong bearish candle
2. A small-bodied candle showing indecision
3. A strong bullish candle

The third candle should close well into the body of the first candle.

---

# Morning Star Psychology

The pattern tells this story:

1. Sellers dominate the first candle.
2. Momentum weakens during the second candle.
3. Buyers take control during the third candle.
4. The market begins rejecting lower prices.
5. A possible bullish reversal develops.

The second candle may be:

- a Doji
- a Spinning Top
- a small bullish candle
- a small bearish candle

Its main purpose is to show hesitation or weakening momentum.

---

# Evening Star

The Evening Star is a three-candle bearish reversal pattern.

It normally appears after an uptrend.

It consists of:

1. A strong bullish candle
2. A small-bodied candle showing indecision
3. A strong bearish candle

The third candle should close well into the body of the first candle.

---

# Evening Star Psychology

The pattern tells this story:

1. Buyers dominate the first candle.
2. Momentum weakens during the second candle.
3. Sellers take control during the third candle.
4. Higher prices are rejected.
5. A possible bearish reversal develops.

The pattern becomes more meaningful near resistance or after an extended rally.

---

# Three White Soldiers

Three White Soldiers is a bullish pattern formed by three strong bullish candles.

Each candle generally:

- closes higher than the previous candle
- opens within or near the previous candle's body
- has a relatively strong body
- shows limited upper wick

It may indicate sustained buying pressure.

The pattern often appears after:

- a downtrend
- a long consolidation
- a market bottom
- a bullish market structure shift

---

# Three White Soldiers Psychology

The pattern shows buyers gaining control over several consecutive periods.

Each candle confirms the strength of the previous one.

However, traders should be careful when the pattern appears after price has already moved too far.

Entering late may create:

- poor risk-to-reward
- a wide stop-loss
- exposure to a pullback
- buying directly into resistance

---

# Three Black Crows

Three Black Crows is a bearish pattern formed by three strong bearish candles.

Each candle generally:

- closes lower than the previous candle
- opens within or near the previous candle's body
- has a strong bearish body
- shows limited lower wick

It may indicate sustained selling pressure.

It often appears after:

- an uptrend
- a long consolidation
- a market top
- a bearish market structure shift

---

# Three Black Crows Psychology

The pattern shows sellers gaining control over several consecutive periods.

Each bearish candle confirms continued selling pressure.

However, entering after the third candle may be risky when price is already near support.

---

# Tweezer Bottom

A Tweezer Bottom is usually formed by two candles with similar lows.

It often appears after a downward move.

The first candle may be bearish.

The second candle may be bullish.

The matching lows suggest that price was rejected from the same area more than once.

This may indicate support.

---

# Tweezer Bottom Psychology

Sellers attempt to push price below a particular level.

Buyers defend that level.

When the market returns to the same low, buyers defend it again.

The repeated rejection may indicate that selling pressure is weakening.

A bullish confirmation candle may strengthen the pattern.

---

# Tweezer Top

A Tweezer Top is usually formed by two candles with similar highs.

It often appears after an upward move.

The first candle may be bullish.

The second candle may be bearish.

The matching highs suggest that price was rejected from the same area more than once.

This may indicate resistance.

---

# Tweezer Top Psychology

Buyers attempt to push price above a particular level.

Sellers defend the level.

When price returns to the same high, sellers reject it again.

The repeated rejection may show that buying pressure is weakening.

---

# Inside Bar Pattern

An Inside Bar forms when the entire range of one candle is contained within the range of the previous candle.

The larger candle is called the Mother Bar.

The smaller candle is called the Inside Bar.

This pattern may indicate:

- consolidation
- indecision
- reduced volatility
- temporary balance
- preparation for a breakout

An Inside Bar is not automatically a reversal pattern.

Its direction depends on context.

---

# Bullish Inside Bar Setup

A bullish Inside Bar setup may become meaningful when it forms:

- during a pullback in an uptrend
- near support
- after bullish rejection
- before a continuation breakout

Confirmation may occur when price breaks above the Mother Bar high.

---

# Bearish Inside Bar Setup

A bearish Inside Bar setup may become meaningful when it forms:

- during a pullback in a downtrend
- near resistance
- after bearish rejection
- before a continuation breakdown

Confirmation may occur when price breaks below the Mother Bar low.

---

# Outside Bar

An Outside Bar forms when the second candle's range is larger than and fully contains the range of the previous candle.

This pattern may show increased volatility and a strong change in control.

A bullish Outside Bar may close strongly upward.

A bearish Outside Bar may close strongly downward.

The pattern is similar to an engulfing pattern, but the full high-to-low range is considered.

---

# Bullish Harami

A Bullish Harami is a two-candle pattern that may appear after a downtrend.

The first candle is a large bearish candle.

The second candle is a small candle whose body remains inside the body of the first candle.

This may indicate that bearish momentum is weakening.

It is not as aggressive as a Bullish Engulfing pattern.

Confirmation is especially important.

---

# Bearish Harami

A Bearish Harami is a two-candle pattern that may appear after an uptrend.

The first candle is a large bullish candle.

The second candle is a small candle whose body remains inside the body of the first candle.

This may indicate that bullish momentum is weakening.

A bearish confirmation candle may improve the setup.

---

# Harami Cross

A Harami Cross occurs when the second candle in a Harami pattern is a Doji.

This may show stronger indecision.

A Bullish Harami Cross may appear after a downtrend.

A Bearish Harami Cross may appear after an uptrend.

The Doji shows that momentum has slowed, but a reversal still requires confirmation.

---

# Reversal Pattern vs Continuation Pattern

Some patterns may act as either reversal or continuation signals depending on context.

For example:

- Inside Bar
- Outside Bar
- Engulfing Pattern
- Doji combinations

A pattern near the end of an extended trend may indicate reversal.

The same pattern during a pullback may indicate continuation.

Context determines interpretation.

---

# The Importance of Market Structure

A candlestick reversal pattern becomes stronger when market structure also changes.

For example, after a downtrend:

1. A Bullish Engulfing pattern forms near support.
2. Price breaks above the previous lower high.
3. A higher low forms.
4. Buyers maintain control.

This provides stronger evidence than the candlestick pattern alone.

For a bearish reversal:

1. A Bearish Engulfing pattern forms near resistance.
2. Price breaks below the previous higher low.
3. A lower high forms.
4. Sellers maintain control.

The market structure shift confirms the possible reversal.

---

# Support and Resistance

Multi-candlestick patterns are more useful when they form at important levels.

A bullish pattern near strong support may have greater meaning.

A bearish pattern near strong resistance may have greater meaning.

Patterns in the middle of a range may produce weaker signals.

Always ask:

- Is there enough space for price to move?
- Is another major level blocking the trade?
- Does the potential target justify the risk?

---

# Higher-Timeframe Alignment

A reversal pattern on a lower timeframe may only represent a small pullback within a larger trend.

For example:

- The 15-minute chart may show a bullish reversal.
- The Daily chart may remain strongly bearish.

This does not mean the lower-timeframe pattern is invalid.

It means the trader must understand that the bullish move may be temporary.

Higher-timeframe analysis helps determine whether a pattern represents:

- a true reversal
- a pullback
- a correction
- a continuation setup

---

# Volume Confirmation

Where reliable volume data is available, increased volume may strengthen a reversal pattern.

For example:

- A Bullish Engulfing candle with increased volume may show stronger buying participation.
- A Bearish Engulfing candle with increased volume may show stronger selling participation.

Low-volume patterns may be less convincing.

Volume should support analysis, not replace it.

---

# Entry Methods

Traders may use different entry methods.

## Immediate Entry

Entering when the reversal pattern closes.

Advantages:

- earlier entry
- potentially better price

Disadvantages:

- less confirmation
- higher chance of false reversal

---

## Confirmation Entry

Entering after price breaks the pattern high or low.

Advantages:

- more evidence
- reduced uncertainty

Disadvantages:

- later entry
- possibly wider stop-loss
- reduced risk-to-reward

---

## Retest Entry

Waiting for price to break and then return to the pattern area.

Advantages:

- potentially better entry
- smaller stop-loss
- improved risk-to-reward

Disadvantages:

- price may not return
- the opportunity may be missed

The trading strategy should define which entry method is allowed.

---

# Stop-Loss Placement

Possible stop-loss locations include:

- below the low of a bullish reversal pattern
- above the high of a bearish reversal pattern
- beyond a support or resistance zone
- beyond the market structure invalidation point

The stop-loss should be placed where the trading idea becomes invalid.

It should not be placed only according to an arbitrary number of points.

---

# Take-Profit Targets

Possible take-profit targets include:

- the next resistance level
- the next support level
- a previous swing high
- a previous swing low
- a fixed risk-to-reward target
- a measured market structure objective

The course recommends generally seeking an average risk-to-reward ratio near:

**1:3**

This means risking one unit to potentially gain three units.

A trade should be rejected when the available target does not justify the risk.

---

# Risk Management

A strong pattern can still fail.

Every trade must include:

- a defined entry
- a defined stop-loss
- a defined target
- calculated position size
- acceptable risk
- a clear invalidation point

A trader should not risk above 5% of total trading capital during one trading session.

Professional traders often use less.

The main objective is capital preservation.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes:

- Trading every engulfing pattern
- Ignoring whether a trend existed before the pattern
- Entering before all candles are completed
- Ignoring support and resistance
- Ignoring higher timeframes
- Calling every large candle an engulfing pattern
- Entering after price has already moved too far
- Using no confirmation
- Placing stops randomly
- Ignoring risk-to-reward
- Risking too much because a pattern looks strong
- Assuming a reversal pattern guarantees a reversal

Candlestick formations provide evidence, not certainty.

---

# A Multi-Candlestick Pattern Checklist

Before trading a pattern, ask:

1. Was there a clear trend or directional move before the pattern?
2. Which pattern has formed?
3. Are all candles completed?
4. Where did the pattern form?
5. Is it near support or resistance?
6. What does the higher timeframe show?
7. Has momentum weakened?
8. Has market structure started to change?
9. Is confirmation present?
10. Where is the invalidation point?
11. Where will the stop-loss be placed?
12. Where is the target?
13. Does the setup offer acceptable risk-to-reward?
14. Does the risk remain within the trading plan?
15. Am I entering based on evidence rather than emotion?

When several conditions are missing, skipping the trade may be the best decision.

---

# Key Principle

A reversal pattern becomes stronger when trend exhaustion, an important price level, market structure change and confirmation all support the same idea.

The pattern alone is never enough.
