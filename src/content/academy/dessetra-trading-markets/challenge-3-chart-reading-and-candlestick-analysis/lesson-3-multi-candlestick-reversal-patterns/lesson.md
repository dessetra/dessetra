# Dessetra Confirmation Model

You have now learned several tools for reading the market.

You can identify:

- trend direction
- market structure
- Support and Resistance
- Supply and Demand
- liquidity areas
- Fair Value Gaps
- multiple-timeframe context
- 21, 50 and 200 EMA
- MACD momentum

The next step is not adding more indicators.

The next step is learning **how to organize the information you already have.**

For this course, we will use the:

## Dessetra Confirmation Model

The sequence is:

**Trend â†’ Structure â†’ Zone â†’ EMA â†’ Momentum â†’ Entry Trigger**

Each step answers a different question.

The objective is to avoid entering a trade simply because one indicator or candle looks attractive.

## Step 1 â€” Trend

First ask:

**What is the dominant market direction?**

Use the higher timeframe to establish context.

From our top-down process:

**H4 â†’ H1 â†’ M15 â†’ M5**

H4 and H1 help establish the broader direction before we look for a lower-timeframe entry.

Classify the market as:

- Bullish
- Bearish
- Ranging

### Bullish Context

Look for:

**Higher Highs + Higher Lows**

### Bearish Context

Look for:

**Lower Highs + Lower Lows**

### Ranging Context

Price repeatedly moves between important support and resistance without maintaining a clear directional structure.

If the trend is unclear, you do not need to force a directional trade.

---

## Step 2 â€” Structure

Trend gives us direction.

Structure tells us whether price behaviour continues to support that direction.

Ask:

- Where are the important swing highs?
- Where are the important swing lows?
- Has price created a Break of Structure?
- Is the trend structure still valid?
- Is price currently moving impulsively or correcting?

### Bullish Example

The higher timeframe is bullish.

Price creates:

**HH â†’ HL â†’ HH**

Then price begins pulling back.

The pullback alone does not automatically make the market bearish.

The important question is whether the bullish structure remains intact.

### Bearish Example

Price creates:

**LL â†’ LH â†’ LL**

Then price begins correcting upward.

That bullish correction does not automatically mean the bearish trend has ended.

Always read the move inside its larger structure.

---

## Step 3 â€” Zone

Once direction and structure are understood, ask:

**Where is price?**

A good idea at a poor location may still produce a poor trade.

Look for important areas such as:

- Support
- Resistance
- Demand
- Supply
- Fair Value Gap
- previous swing high
- previous swing low
- liquidity area

We are interested in how price behaves when it reaches an important area.

---

## Why Location Matters

Imagine the market is bullish.

The trend is correct.

The structure is bullish.

But price has already moved strongly upward and is now directly below major resistance.

Buying simply because the market is bullish may provide poor risk-to-reward.

Compare that with price pulling back toward a Demand Zone while bullish structure remains intact.

The second location may provide a more logical area to begin looking for confirmation.

The model therefore asks:

**Trend first. Location before entry.**

---

## Step 4 â€” EMA

Now check the **21, 50 and 200 EMA**.

The EMA should help confirm what price structure is already showing.

### Bullish Alignment

**21 EMA > 50 EMA > 200 EMA**

Ideally:

- the averages are not heavily compressed
- they generally point upward
- price structure is also bullish

### Bearish Alignment

**21 EMA < 50 EMA < 200 EMA**

Ideally:

- the averages generally point downward
- price structure is bearish
- price is not simply ranging through the averages

---

## EMA Pullback

During a trend, price may correct toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA.

This can become interesting when the EMA area overlaps with another important price area.

For example:

- H1 bullish structure
- price enters H1 Demand
- 50 EMA is near the same area
- sellers begin losing momentum

This creates **confluence**.

But remember:

**EMA touch â‰  automatic entry.**

We still need momentum and an entry trigger.

---

## Step 5 â€” Momentum

Next ask:

**Is momentum beginning to support the trade direction?**

Use MACD as one confirmation tool.

### Bullish Momentum Confirmation

You may observe:

- MACD Line crossing above Signal Line
- bullish histogram expansion
- bearish momentum weakening
- bullish momentum beginning after a pullback

### Bearish Momentum Confirmation

You may observe:

- MACD Line crossing below Signal Line
- bearish histogram expansion
- bullish momentum weakening
- bearish momentum returning after a correction

MACD should support the setup.

It should not create the setup.

---

## Step 6 â€” Entry Trigger

Even when the previous conditions agree, we still need a reason to execute.

The **Entry Trigger** is the final evidence that price is responding as expected.

Possible triggers include:

- rejection from an important zone
- strong confirmation candle
- lower-timeframe structure break
- breakout and retest
- rejection followed by continuation
- completed candle confirming the expected direction

The exact trigger should be visible and explainable.

Avoid entering because:

**"It looks like it is about to move."**

Instead, identify what price has actually done.

---

## Wait for the Candle to Close

A candle can look strongly bullish or bearish while it is forming and change completely before it closes.

The same applies to indicator readings.

For setups that depend on candle confirmation, evaluate the **completed candle**.

This reduces the temptation to enter based on temporary intrabar movement.

---

## The Complete Sequence

Before considering an entry, move through the model in order:

## 1. Trend
What is the dominant direction?

## 2. Structure
Does market structure support that direction?

## 3. Zone
Is price at a meaningful location?

## 4. EMA
Do the 21, 50 and 200 EMA support the analysis?

## 5. Momentum
Does MACD support the expected movement?

## 6. Entry Trigger
Has price actually confirmed the setup?

This gives us:

**Trend â†’ Structure â†’ Zone â†’ EMA â†’ Momentum â†’ Entry Trigger**

Assume EURUSD is being analysed.

### Trend

### Structure
H1 shows:

**HH â†’ HL â†’ HH**

Price begins correcting toward the previous Higher Low area.

### Zone
The correction reaches an H1 Demand Zone.

### EMA

**21 > 50 > 200**

### Entry Trigger

That does not guarantee a winning trade.

It means the setup has passed the confirmation process.

## When Conditions Do Not Agree

You will often find incomplete setups.

For example:

### Scenario A

- Trend = Bullish
- Structure = Bullish
- Zone = Demand
- EMA = Bullish
- MACD = Bearish
- Entry Trigger = None

**Decision: WAIT**

The setup has not completed.

---

### Scenario B

- Trend = Bearish
- Structure = Bearish
- Zone = Supply
- EMA = Bearish
- Momentum = Bearish
- Entry Trigger = Bearish confirmation

**Decision: Setup may proceed to risk evaluation.**

Notice that we still have not automatically entered.

Entry, Stop Loss, Take Profit and risk-to-reward must still make sense.

That comes in Lesson 4.

---

### Scenario C

- Trend = Unclear
- Structure = Ranging
- EMA = Compressed
- MACD = Repeated crossovers

**Decision: NO TRADE**

There is no requirement to trade simply because the market is open.

---

## Approve, Wait or Reject

For practice, classify setups into three categories.

### APPROVE FOR RISK EVALUATION

The confirmation sequence is sufficiently aligned.

This does **not** mean enter immediately.

It means the setup can proceed to entry, SL, TP and R:R evaluation.

### WAIT

The idea may be valid, but confirmation is incomplete.

### REJECT

Important conditions conflict or the setup does not follow the trading plan.

Learning to reject poor setups is as important as finding good ones.

---

## The Dessetra Checklist

Before moving a setup to risk evaluation, ask:

**TREND**
- Is H4/H1 bullish, bearish or ranging?

**STRUCTURE**
- Are the important highs and lows clear?
- Is structure supporting my direction?

**ZONE**
- Is price at a meaningful area?
- Am I entering directly into an opposing level?

**EMA**
- What is the 21/50/200 alignment?
- Are the EMAs trending or compressed?

**MOMENTUM**
- Does MACD support the direction?
- Is momentum strengthening or weakening?

**TRIGGER**
- What exactly confirmed my entry?
- Has the confirmation candle closed?

If you cannot clearly explain the setup, do not force it.

## Key Takeaways

- Do not build trades from one indicator.
- Start with higher-timeframe direction.
- Confirm that market structure supports the direction.
- Evaluate where price is located.
- Use 21/50/200 EMA as additional trend confirmation.
- Use MACD to evaluate momentum.
- Require a clear entry trigger.
- Use completed candles when candle-close confirmation is required.
- Conflicting evidence can mean **WAIT**.
- Poor or unclear conditions can mean **REJECT**.
- A confirmed setup still needs proper risk evaluation before execution.
