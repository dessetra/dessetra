# Chart Patterns & Price Action Basics

Price action is the study of how price moves on a chart without relying heavily on indicators.

Professional traders often believe that price itself contains the most important information.

Chart patterns are recurring formations created by price movement.

They reflect the continuous struggle between buyers and sellers.

Neither chart patterns nor price action predict the future.

Instead, they help traders identify situations where one outcome appears more likely than another.

---

# What Is Price Action?

Price action refers to the movement of price over time.

Instead of depending on many indicators, price action traders study:

- market structure
- trends
- support
- resistance
- candlesticks
- momentum
- breakouts
- pullbacks

The objective is to understand what buyers and sellers are doing.

---

# Why Price Action Matters

Indicators are usually based on historical price.

Price action is the source from which many indicators are calculated.

Understanding price action helps traders:

- react more quickly
- avoid indicator overload
- understand market psychology
- identify high-probability setups
- improve timing

---

# Swing Highs and Swing Lows

Markets move in waves.

A **Swing High** is a temporary peak where price stops rising and begins to fall.

A **Swing Low** is a temporary bottom where price stops falling and begins to rise.

Swing points help traders identify:

- trend direction
- market structure
- support
- resistance

---

# Market Structure Review

Market structure is the foundation of price action.

### Bullish Structure

Characteristics:

- Higher Highs (HH)
- Higher Lows (HL)

Buyers remain in control.

---

### Bearish Structure

Characteristics:

- Lower Highs (LH)
- Lower Lows (LL)

Sellers remain in control.

---

### Range Structure

Characteristics:

- Price moves between support and resistance.
- No clear trend exists.
- Buyers and sellers share control.

Range markets require different strategies from trending markets.

---

# Break of Structure (BOS)

A Break of Structure occurs when price breaks an important swing point.

Examples:

Bullish BOS:

Price breaks above the previous Higher High.

Bearish BOS:

Price breaks below the previous Lower Low.

A BOS may indicate that momentum is continuing.

However, traders should wait for confirmation instead of reacting immediately.

---

# Change of Character (CHOCH)

A Change of Character occurs when market structure begins to shift.

For example:

A downtrend creates:

- Lower High
- Lower Low

Then price breaks above the previous Lower High.

This may be an early sign that buyers are gaining control.

Likewise, in an uptrend, a break below the previous Higher Low may signal increasing seller strength.

CHOCH is an early warning—not confirmation of a new trend.

---

# Support and Resistance Revisited

Support is an area where buyers have previously entered the market.

Resistance is an area where sellers have previously entered the market.

Remember:

Support and resistance are usually zones rather than exact prices.

Price may:

- bounce
- break
- temporarily move beyond the zone before reversing

Always look for confirmation.

---

# Trendlines

Trendlines connect important swing highs or swing lows.

An upward trendline connects Higher Lows.

A downward trendline connects Lower Highs.

Trendlines help identify:

- trend direction
- pullbacks
- possible breakout areas

They are guides, not guarantees.

---

# Channels

A channel forms when price moves between two roughly parallel trendlines.

### Ascending Channel

Price generally moves upward while respecting both trendlines.

---

### Descending Channel

Price generally moves downward inside the channel.

---

### Horizontal Channel

Price moves sideways between support and resistance.

Channels help traders identify possible continuation or reversal areas.

---

# Triangle Patterns

Triangles develop when price gradually compresses.

Volatility often decreases before a breakout.

---

## Ascending Triangle

Characteristics:

- Flat resistance
- Rising support

Often suggests increasing buying pressure.

---

## Descending Triangle

Characteristics:

- Flat support
- Falling resistance

Often suggests increasing selling pressure.

---

## Symmetrical Triangle

Characteristics:

- Falling resistance
- Rising support

Neither buyers nor sellers have full control.

A breakout may occur in either direction.

---

# Rectangle Pattern

A rectangle forms when price repeatedly bounces between horizontal support and resistance.

It represents consolidation.

Eventually price may:

- break upward
- break downward

Confirmation is needed before entering.

---

# Wedge Patterns

A wedge is formed when both trendlines move in the same direction while converging.

---

## Rising Wedge

Price rises but momentum weakens.

May indicate possible bearish reversal or continuation depending on context.

---

## Falling Wedge

Price falls while selling pressure weakens.

May indicate possible bullish reversal or continuation depending on context.

---

# Double Top

A Double Top forms when price reaches approximately the same high twice before declining.

It may indicate:

- weakening buyers
- increasing seller pressure
- possible bearish reversal

Confirmation usually comes after price breaks below the neckline.

---

# Double Bottom

A Double Bottom forms when price reaches approximately the same low twice before rising.

It may indicate:

- weakening sellers
- increasing buyer strength
- possible bullish reversal

Confirmation usually comes after price breaks above the neckline.

---

# Triple Top

A Triple Top consists of three similar highs.

Repeated rejection suggests buyers are struggling to move higher.

Confirmation comes after support breaks.

---

# Triple Bottom

A Triple Bottom consists of three similar lows.

Repeated rejection suggests sellers are struggling to move lower.

Confirmation comes after resistance breaks.

---

# Head and Shoulders

The Head and Shoulders pattern is one of the most recognized bearish reversal patterns.

It consists of:

- Left Shoulder
- Head
- Right Shoulder
- Neckline

The pattern suggests buyers are gradually losing strength.

Confirmation usually occurs after the neckline breaks.

---

# Inverse Head and Shoulders

This is the bullish opposite of the Head and Shoulders pattern.

It consists of:

- Left Shoulder
- Head
- Right Shoulder
- Neckline

It may indicate that sellers are losing control and buyers are taking over.

Confirmation usually occurs after the neckline breaks upward.

---

# Flags

Flags are short consolidation patterns that often develop after strong momentum.

---

## Bull Flag

A strong bullish move is followed by a small downward or sideways consolidation.

If buyers regain control, the trend may continue upward.

---

## Bear Flag

A strong bearish move is followed by a small upward or sideways consolidation.

If sellers regain control, the trend may continue downward.

---

# Pennants

Pennants resemble small symmetrical triangles following strong market movement.

They often indicate temporary consolidation before continuation.

As with all patterns, confirmation is essential.

---

# Consolidation

Consolidation occurs when price pauses before making its next significant move.

During consolidation:

- volatility decreases
- buyers and sellers reach temporary balance
- breakouts become more likely

Not every consolidation leads to a strong move.

---

# Breakouts

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond an important level.

Possible breakout areas include:

- support
- resistance
- trendlines
- channels
- triangles
- rectangles

Not every breakout succeeds.

---

# False Breakouts

Sometimes price briefly breaks an important level before reversing.

This is called a false breakout.

Professional traders often wait for:

- candle close
- confirmation
- retest

before entering.

---

# Pullbacks

A pullback is a temporary move against the main trend.

Example:

In an uptrend, price may temporarily fall before continuing upward.

Many traders prefer entering during pullbacks rather than chasing price.

---

# Price Action Checklist

Before entering a trade, consider:

- What is the overall trend?
- Where is price located?
- Is there a chart pattern?
- Is market structure bullish or bearish?
- Has price confirmed the breakout?
- Is there enough room to the next support or resistance?
- Does the setup provide at least a 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio?
- Does the trade follow my trading plan?

---

# Combining Everything

Professional traders rarely depend on one signal alone.

A stronger setup may combine:

- Higher-timeframe trend
- Support or resistance
- Market structure
- Candlestick confirmation
- Chart pattern
- Multiple timeframe alignment
- Proper risk management

The more independent factors supporting the same idea, the stronger the setup may become.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes:

- Trading every pattern you see
- Ignoring the higher timeframe
- Drawing trendlines through random points
- Assuming every breakout will continue
- Chasing price after large candles
- Ignoring confirmation
- Entering without a stop-loss
- Risking too much on one trade

Successful trading comes from consistency, not excitement.

---

# Key Principle

Chart patterns and price action help traders understand what the market is communicating.

They should be used together with market structure, support and resistance, multiple timeframe analysis and sound risk management.

No pattern guarantees success.
