# Complete Trade Setup

A good market analysis is not yet a complete trade.

You may correctly identify:

- the trend
- market structure
- an important zone
- EMA alignment
- momentum
- an entry trigger

But before executing, you still need to answer:

**Where exactly will I enter?**

**Where is my analysis invalid?**

**Where should my Stop Loss go?**

**Where is my Take Profit?**

**Does the potential reward justify the risk?**

This lesson turns the Dessetra Confirmation Model into a complete trade plan.

## 1. Define the Entry

Your **Entry** is the price or condition at which you intend to open the trade.

An entry should come from your trading rules.

Possible entry methods include:

- confirmation candle close
- break of a lower-timeframe structure
- breakout and retest
- rejection from an important zone
- pending order at a predefined area

The important point is consistency.

Avoid entering simply because price is moving quickly.

---

## Confirmation Entry

A confirmation entry waits for additional evidence before execution.

For example:

Price reaches Demand.

Instead of immediately buying, the trader waits for:

- bullish rejection
- bullish momentum
- lower-timeframe structure change
- completed confirmation candle

The advantage is that more evidence is available.

The disadvantage is that the entry may occur later.

---

## Retest Entry

Sometimes price breaks an important level and then returns to test that area.

For example:

Resistance breaks.

Price moves above it.

Price returns toward the broken area.

The former resistance may now behave as support.

If the setup remains valid and confirmation appears, the retest can provide an entry opportunity.

A retest is not guaranteed.

Price may break and continue without returning.

---

## 2. Define the Invalidation Point

Before thinking about profit, ask:

**What price behaviour would prove that my trade idea is no longer valid?**

This is the **invalidation point**.

Example:

You want to buy because price has reacted from Demand and maintained a Higher Low.

If price breaks clearly below the structural level that your bullish idea depends on, the setup may no longer be valid.

The invalidation point comes from the market logic behind the trade.

---

## Why Invalidation Comes Before Stop Loss

A common mistake is deciding:

**"I will use a 20-pip Stop Loss."**

and then placing it somewhere simply because it is 20 pips away.

Instead, first determine:

**Where does my analysis become wrong?**

Then place the Stop Loss according to that structure while accounting for the trading plan and position size.

The market determines the logical invalidation area.

Your risk rules determine how much capital you can expose to it.

---

## 3. Place the Stop Loss

A **Stop Loss (SL)** is the protective exit used if price moves against the trade.

Possible logical locations include:

### Bullish Setup

- below the relevant swing low
- below the Demand/Support area
- beyond the structure that invalidates the bullish idea

### Bearish Setup

- above the relevant swing high
- above the Supply/Resistance area
- beyond the structure that invalidates the bearish idea

The Stop Loss should not be moved farther away simply because the trade is losing.

---

## Stop Loss Example

Suppose:

- H1 trend is bullish
- price pulls back into Demand
- bullish structure remains valid
- M5 gives bullish confirmation

The relevant M5/H1 swing low may define the invalidation area.

If price breaks that structure decisively, the reason for the trade may no longer exist.

The Stop Loss can therefore be positioned beyond the logical invalidation area according to the strategy rules.

---

## 4. Define the Take Profit

The **Take Profit (TP)** defines where the trade will close in profit if price reaches the planned target.

Possible targets include:

- previous swing high
- previous swing low
- Support
- Resistance
- Supply
- Demand
- liquidity area
- predetermined risk-to-reward objective

Your target should make sense within the actual market structure.

---

## Do Not Ignore Obstacles

Suppose you want to buy.

Your setup appears strong, but major resistance is immediately above the entry.

Even if your indicators are bullish, there may not be enough room for price to reach a reasonable target.

Ask:

**What stands between my entry and target?**

A technically valid direction can still produce a poor trade location.

---

## 5. Calculate Risk-to-Reward

Risk-to-Reward compares:

**how much you are risking**

with

**how much you could potentially gain.**

It is written:

**Risk : Reward**

For example:

### 1:1

Risk $10 to potentially gain $10.

### 1:2

Risk $10 to potentially gain $20.

### 1:3

Risk $10 to potentially gain $30.

---

## Dessetra Target Framework

Throughout this course, **1:3 R:R** is our preferred planning benchmark when market structure provides enough room.

Example:

Entry: 1.1000

Stop Loss: 1.0980

Risk distance: 20 pips

A 1:3 target would require approximately:

**60 pips potential reward**

But do not force a 1:3 target through major market structure simply to make the numbers work.

The chart must provide enough logical room.

---

## When to Reject a Trade Because of R:R

Imagine a Buy setup where:

- Entry is valid
- Stop Loss requires 30 pips
- major Resistance is only 35 pips above the entry

There is little available reward relative to the required risk.

Moving the Stop Loss artificially closer just to create a better ratio may damage the logic of the setup.

Moving the target unrealistically beyond major resistance does not improve the actual opportunity either.

Sometimes the correct decision is:

**REJECT THE TRADE.**

---

## 6. Check the Confirmation Candle

If your strategy requires candle-close confirmation, wait until the candle closes.

An unfinished candle can change dramatically.

For example:

A candle may appear strongly bullish during its formation.

Before closing, sellers may push price back down and leave a large upper wick.

The completed candle provides more reliable information about what happened during that period.

Do not assume how a candle will close.

### R:R

## A Setup Can Be Correct but Still Be Rejected

This is an important trading lesson.

You may correctly identify the direction but still decide not to trade.

Examples:

- Stop Loss is too large for the available target.
- Major Support/Resistance blocks the target.
- Confirmation arrived too late.
- Price has already moved too far.
- Market structure changed before entry.
- R:R is unacceptable.
- The setup no longer follows your trading plan.

Good analysis does not require you to enter.

---

## Never Chase the Entry

Suppose your planned entry was near Demand.

Price confirms and then moves strongly away before you execute.

Entering much later changes:

- entry price
- Stop Loss distance
- potential reward
- R:R

The original setup may no longer exist.

Recalculate the trade from the **current price**.

If it no longer meets your rules:

**LET IT GO.**

There will be another setup.

## Final Pre-Trade Checklist

Before executing, answer every question.

### MARKET

- What is the higher-timeframe trend?
- Does structure support my direction?
- Is price at an important zone?

### CONFIRMATION

- What is the 21/50/200 EMA alignment?
- Does MACD support the setup?
- What is my exact entry trigger?
- Has the required candle closed?

### RISK

- What is my entry?
- Where is my invalidation point?
- Where is my Stop Loss?
- Where is my Take Profit?
- What is my R:R?
- Is there enough room before the next major obstacle?

### DECISION

**EXECUTE**

**WAIT**

or

**REJECT**

Do not invent reasons to enter after completing the checklist.

## Key Takeaways

- Confirmation alone does not make a complete trade.
- Define Entry, Invalidation, SL and TP before execution.
- Place the Stop Loss where the trade idea becomes invalidâ€”not at a random distance.
- Targets should respect actual market structure.
- Risk-to-Reward compares potential loss with potential gain.
- Dessetra generally seeks approximately **1:3 R:R when market structure allows it**.

- Wait for completed candles when candle-close confirmation is required.
- Do not chase a missed entry.
- A valid market idea can still be an unacceptable trade.
- **Execute, Wait and Reject are all valid trading decisions.**
