# Trading Trendlines

Trendlines are one of the simplest yet most effective tools for understanding market direction.

The Dessetra Trendline Method is based on one simple idea:

**Trade with the trend, not against it.**

A trendline allows traders to visualize the path of the market and identify areas where price may react before continuing in the existing trend.

Unlike the Moving Average Rule, which is mainly designed for the 5-minute timeframe, the Trendline Method performs best on:

- 15-Minute
- 1-Hour
- 4-Hour

These timeframes usually produce cleaner trend structures and more reliable trendlines.

---

# What is a Trendline?

A trendline is a straight line drawn to connect important turning points in the market.

The purpose is to identify the current direction of price movement and possible future reaction zones.

Trendlines should be viewed as dynamic support or resistance rather than exact prices.

---

# Trendlines Follow Market Structure

Markets rarely move in straight lines.

Instead, they move in waves.

During an uptrend:

- price rises
- retraces
- rises again
- retraces again

During a downtrend:

- price falls
- retraces
- falls again
- retraces again

Trendlines help traders visualize these waves.

---

# Drawing an Uptrend Trendline

For an uptrend:

Open your chart and select the **Trendline Tool**.

Identify at least two important support points (higher lows).

Connect those support points using a straight line.

The line should extend into the future.

This line now represents dynamic support.

As long as price continues respecting the trendline, the uptrend remains valid.

---

# Minimum Number of Touches

Although two points are required to draw a trendline, a third touch greatly increases confidence.

Generally:

- First point establishes the line.
- Second point confirms the line.
- Third reaction provides stronger market confirmation.

The more genuine reactions a trendline produces without breaking, the more attention it deserves.

---

# Buying from an Uptrend Trendline

Once the trendline has been established, patience becomes important.

Do not buy immediately after drawing the line.

Instead:

Wait for the market to retrace back toward the trendline.

As price approaches the trendline, begin looking for confirmation that buyers are defending the area.

Possible confirmations include:

- bullish rejection candle
- bullish engulfing candle
- strong bullish momentum
- higher low formation
- break of minor bearish structure

Once confirmation appears, a buy position may be considered.

---

# Example of an Uptrend Trade

Suppose the market has been making:

- Higher highs
- Higher lows

You connect two higher lows with a trendline.

Price later retraces toward that line.

The trendline holds.

A bullish rejection candle forms.

The market begins moving upward again.

This creates a potential buying opportunity.

---

# Drawing a Downtrend Trendline

For a bearish market:

Select the Trendline Tool.

Identify at least two important resistance points (lower highs).

Connect those resistance points using a straight line.

Extend the line forward.

The line now acts as dynamic resistance.

As long as price respects the trendline, the bearish trend remains valid.

---

# Selling from a Downtrend Trendline

After drawing the trendline:

Do not sell immediately.

Wait patiently for price to retrace upward toward the trendline.

As price approaches the line, watch for confirmation that sellers are becoming active.

Possible confirmations include:

- bearish rejection candle
- bearish engulfing candle
- lower high formation
- strong bearish momentum
- break of minor bullish structure

Once confirmation appears, a sell position may be considered.

---

# Example of a Sell Trade

Suppose price has been making:

- Lower highs
- Lower lows

A trendline is drawn connecting two lower highs.

Price later retraces upward toward the trendline.

A bearish rejection candle appears.

The market resumes moving downward.

This creates a potential selling opportunity.

---

# Trendlines Are Dynamic Support and Resistance

Unlike horizontal support and resistance, trendlines move together with price.

An uptrend trendline acts as dynamic support.

A downtrend trendline acts as dynamic resistance.

Because markets continue moving, these reaction areas also change over time.

---

# Timeframe Selection

The Dessetra Trendline Method performs best on:

## 15-Minute

Useful for intraday trading.

Produces many quality opportunities.

---

## 1-Hour

Produces cleaner market structure.

Less market noise.

Suitable for traders who prefer fewer but stronger setups.

---

## 4-Hour

Best for identifying major market trends.

Signals occur less frequently but often carry greater significance.

---

# Avoid Very Small Timeframes

Trendlines on extremely small timeframes may produce many false signals because of market noise.

The Dessetra methodology therefore prefers:

- 15-Minute
- 1-Hour
- 4-Hour

---

# Trendline Breaks

Eventually every trend ends.

One sign is a clear trendline break.

However, a trendline break alone does not automatically mean the trend has reversed.

Sometimes price briefly breaks the trendline before returning to the original direction.

Always wait for additional confirmation.

---

# False Breakouts

Markets sometimes move slightly beyond a trendline before reversing.

These are known as false breakouts.

Avoid reacting immediately.

Allow the market to reveal whether the break is genuine.

Patience reduces unnecessary losses.

---

# Combine Trendlines with Market Structure

A trendline should never be analysed alone.

Always consider:

- Higher highs
- Higher lows
- Lower highs
- Lower lows

If market structure agrees with the trendline, confidence increases.

---

# Combine Trendlines with Other Dessetra Methods

The Trendline Method becomes even stronger when combined with the earlier methodologies taught in this course.

Examples include:

- Trendline + 21 EMA
- Trendline + 50 EMA

- Trendline + Support Zone
- Trendline + Resistance Zone

When several independent factors support the same trade idea, confidence in the setup generally improves.

---

# Risk Management

Even perfect-looking trendlines can fail.

Every trade should therefore include:

- planned stop-loss
- calculated position size
- realistic target

For this course:

**Total trading session risk must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

---

# Profit Targets

Profit targets should be based on market structure.

Possible targets include:

- previous swing high
- previous swing low
- next support
- next resistance

Whenever technically realistic, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- drawing trendlines through random candles
- forcing a trendline where none exists
- trading immediately after drawing the line
- ignoring confirmation
- trading against the trend
- using very small timeframes
- ignoring market structure
- risking more than planned
- assuming every trendline will hold

---

# Dessetra Trendline Checklist

Before entering a trade, confirm:

1. Am I using the 15-Minute, 1-Hour or 4-Hour timeframe?
2. Is the trendline drawn using at least two clear support or resistance points?
3. Has the market already respected the trendline?
4. Is price retracing toward the trendline?
5. Has confirmation appeared?
6. Does market structure agree with the trendline?
7. Is my stop-loss technically placed?
8. Is my target realistic?
9. Does the trade support approximately 1:3 risk-to-reward?
10. Will my total session risk remain below 5%?

If any important condition is missing, continue waiting.

---

# Key Principle

The Dessetra Trendline Method is built on patience.

Instead of chasing the market, traders allow price to return naturally to the trendline before looking for confirmation.

This produces more disciplined entries and better risk management.

For a downtrend:

- Connect at least two resistance points (lower highs).
- Wait for price to retrace back to the trendline.
- Look for bearish confirmation before considering a sell.

This methodology works best on:

- 15-Minute
- 1-Hour
- 4-Hour
