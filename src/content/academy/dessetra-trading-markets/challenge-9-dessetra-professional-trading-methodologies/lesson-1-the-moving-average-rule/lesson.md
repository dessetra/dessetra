# The Moving Average Rule

The Moving Average Rule is one of the core Dessetra trading methodologies.

It uses three Exponential Moving Averages, also known as EMAs, to help traders:

- identify trend direction
- understand market alignment
- recognise retracements
- locate possible entry zones
- avoid trading against the dominant trend

This method is especially suitable for scalping on the 5-minute timeframe.

The objective is not to enter simply because price touches a moving average.

The trader must first confirm that the EMAs are correctly aligned and that the market is clearly trending.

---

# What Is an Exponential Moving Average?

An Exponential Moving Average is a technical indicator that tracks the average price of a market over a selected number of periods.

Unlike a Simple Moving Average, an EMA gives more importance to recent price movement.

This allows it to respond more quickly when market conditions change.

In this methodology, three EMAs are used together.

---

# The Three EMAs

The Moving Average Rule uses:

- 21 EMA
- 50 EMA
- 200 EMA

Each EMA has a specific role.

---

# EMA Configuration

Configure the indicators as follows:

## 21 EMA

- Length: 21
- Colour: Red
- Thickness: Thinnest of the three

The 21 EMA reacts quickly to recent price movement.

It is the fastest EMA in the setup.

---

## 50 EMA

- Length: 50
- Colour: Yellow
- Thickness: Medium

The 50 EMA represents the medium-term direction of the market.

It is slower than the 21 EMA but faster than the 200 EMA.

---

## 200 EMA

- Length: 200
- Colour: Blue
- Thickness: Thickest of the three

The 200 EMA shows the broader market direction.

It is the slowest EMA in the setup.

---

# Increasing Line Thickness

The three EMAs should be configured with increasing thickness.

This means:

- 21 EMA should be the thinnest
- 50 EMA should be thicker than the 21 EMA
- 200 EMA should be the thickest

This makes the indicators easier to recognise on the chart.

It also helps the trader quickly distinguish the fast, medium and slow moving averages.

---

# Recommended Timeframe

This method is most appropriate for scalping on the:

**5-minute timeframe**

The 5-minute chart allows the trader to observe:

- short-term trend direction
- intraday pullbacks
- reactions around the EMAs
- possible scalp entries

The methodology may be observed on other timeframes, but the Dessetra Moving Average Rule is primarily taught here as a 5-minute scalping method.

---

# The Basic Principle

The method follows a simple principle:

**Trade in the direction of the EMA alignment and wait for price to retrace toward the moving averages before considering an entry.**

The EMAs should not be randomly arranged.

They must appear in the correct order.

---

# Uptrend Alignment

The market is considered to be in a valid bullish alignment when:

1. The 21 EMA is at the top.
2. The 50 EMA is below the 21 EMA.
3. The 200 EMA is below both the 21 EMA and the 50 EMA.

The correct order is:

**21 EMA → 50 EMA → 200 EMA**

From top to bottom:

- Red 21 EMA
- Yellow 50 EMA
- Blue 200 EMA

This arrangement shows that short-term price movement is stronger than the medium-term average, while both remain above the broader long-term average.

---

# Visual Structure of an Uptrend

A clear uptrend should display:

- price trading mostly above the EMAs
- 21 EMA above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA above 200 EMA
- moving averages generally pointing upward
- visible separation between the EMAs

The clearer the alignment, the stronger the trend structure may appear.

---

# Bullish Entry Principle

In an uptrend, the trader should not chase price while it is already moving far above the EMAs.

The preferred approach is to wait for price to retrace.

A retracement is a temporary movement against the main trend.

In a bullish market, this means price moves downward toward one or more of the EMAs.

---

# Preferred Bullish Entry Areas

The most important retracement zones are:

- 21 EMA
- 50 EMA

The 21 EMA is usually the first area of interest.

The 50 EMA may become important when the retracement is deeper.

The 200 EMA may also act as a reaction area, but a retracement that reaches it should be analysed more carefully because it may indicate a weaker trend or changing market structure.

---

# Bullish Entry Example

Assume the 5-minute chart shows:

- 21 EMA above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA above 200 EMA
- all three EMAs pointing upward
- price trading above the EMAs

Price then pulls back toward the 21 EMA.

Instead of entering immediately, the trader waits for evidence that the retracement may be ending.

Possible confirmation may include:

- bullish rejection
- bullish candlestick formation
- market structure holding
- momentum returning upward
- price failing to close strongly below the EMA

The trader may then consider a buy entry in the direction of the existing uptrend.

---

# Downtrend Alignment

The market is considered to be in a valid bearish alignment when:

1. The 21 EMA is at the bottom.
2. The 50 EMA is above the 21 EMA.
3. The 200 EMA is above both the 21 EMA and the 50 EMA.

The correct order is:

**200 EMA → 50 EMA → 21 EMA**

From top to bottom:

- Blue 200 EMA
- Yellow 50 EMA
- Red 21 EMA

This shows that short-term price movement is weaker than the medium-term average, while both remain below the broader long-term average.

---

# Visual Structure of a Downtrend

A clear downtrend should display:

- price trading mostly below the EMAs
- 21 EMA below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA below 200 EMA
- moving averages generally pointing downward
- visible separation between the EMAs

The clearer the bearish alignment, the stronger the downtrend structure may appear.

---

# Bearish Entry Principle

In a downtrend, the trader should avoid selling after price has already moved too far below the EMAs.

The preferred approach is to wait for price to retrace upward.

This allows the trader to look for an entry closer to the moving averages rather than chasing the market.

---

# Preferred Bearish Entry Areas

The most important retracement zones are:

- 21 EMA
- 50 EMA

The 21 EMA is usually the first area of interest.

The 50 EMA may become important when the retracement is deeper.

A retracement toward the 200 EMA should be treated carefully because it may indicate weakening bearish momentum or a possible structural change.

---

# Bearish Entry Example

Assume the 5-minute chart shows:

- 21 EMA below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA below 200 EMA
- all three EMAs pointing downward
- price trading below the EMAs

Price then retraces upward toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA.

The trader waits for confirmation that the retracement may be ending.

Possible confirmation may include:

- bearish rejection
- bearish candlestick formation
- market structure remaining bearish
- momentum returning downward
- price failing to close strongly above the EMA

The trader may then consider a sell entry in the direction of the existing downtrend.

---

# Why Retracement Entries Are Preferred

Entering after a retracement may provide:

- a better entry price
- a clearer stop-loss location
- improved risk-to-reward
- less need to chase the market
- greater alignment with the existing trend

However, a retracement does not automatically guarantee that the trend will continue.

Confirmation remains important.

---

# The Role of the 21 EMA

The 21 EMA is the fastest indicator in the setup.

It follows price closely and is often the first moving average reached during a shallow retracement.

It is commonly used to identify:

- short-term trend strength
- shallow pullbacks
- early continuation opportunities

In a strong trend, price may repeatedly react around the 21 EMA.

---

# The Role of the 50 EMA

The 50 EMA is slower than the 21 EMA.

It often becomes important during deeper retracements.

It may help identify:

- medium-depth pullbacks
- stronger reaction zones
- possible continuation after temporary weakness

When price reaches the 50 EMA, the trader should examine whether the wider trend structure remains valid.

---

# The Role of the 200 EMA

The 200 EMA represents the broadest trend in this setup.

It helps the trader recognise the overall market direction.

In an uptrend:

- price and the faster EMAs should normally remain above it

In a downtrend:

- price and the faster EMAs should normally remain below it

The 200 EMA may also act as a major dynamic support or resistance area.

---

# Dynamic Support and Resistance

Moving averages can behave like dynamic support and resistance.

Unlike fixed horizontal levels, they move as new price data appears.

In an uptrend:

- the 21 EMA or 50 EMA may act as dynamic support

In a downtrend:

- the 21 EMA or 50 EMA may act as dynamic resistance

These areas should be treated as zones rather than exact points.

---

# Do Not Enter on Touch Alone

A common beginner mistake is entering immediately when price touches an EMA.

A touch alone is not enough.

Price may:

- break through the EMA
- continue retracing
- move sideways
- reverse the entire trend

The trader should wait for confirmation that the EMA zone is being respected.

---

# Valid Confirmation

Possible confirmation may include:

- bullish or bearish rejection candles
- engulfing candles
- break of minor structure
- continuation pattern
- momentum returning in the trend direction
- price closing back in the direction of the main trend

The exact confirmation should be consistent with the trader's written rules.

---

# Avoid Trading When the EMAs Are Mixed

Do not apply this method when the EMAs are arranged randomly.

Examples of weak conditions include:

- 21 EMA crossing repeatedly above and below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA crossing around 200 EMA
- all three EMAs moving sideways
- price constantly cutting through all three EMAs
- little separation between the moving averages

These conditions may indicate consolidation or an unclear market direction.

---

# Avoid Flat Moving Averages

When all three EMAs are flat, the market may lack clear momentum.

Flat EMAs often appear during:

- consolidation
- ranging conditions
- low volatility
- uncertain direction

The Moving Average Rule works best when the EMAs have a clear direction and proper separation.

---

# Avoid Late Entries

Do not enter when price has moved too far away from the EMAs.

A late entry may produce:

- poor risk-to-reward
- wide stop-loss requirements
- increased chance of entering before a retracement
- emotional pressure

The correct approach is to wait for price to return toward the moving averages.

---

# Avoid Trading Every EMA Touch

Not every touch of the 21 EMA or 50 EMA is a valid opportunity.

The trader must consider:

- overall alignment
- slope of the EMAs
- market structure
- strength of the retracement
- candlestick confirmation
- nearby support and resistance
- current volatility

The setup should be complete before entry.

---

# Stop-Loss Placement

The stop-loss should be placed at the point where the trade idea becomes invalid.

Possible areas include:

- below the retracement low in a bullish setup
- above the retracement high in a bearish setup
- beyond nearby market structure
- beyond a technically meaningful level

The stop-loss should not be placed randomly.

---

# Take-Profit Planning

The profit target should be based on market structure and a realistic continuation objective.

Possible target areas include:

- previous high
- previous low
- next resistance
- next support
- measured continuation target

For this course, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

This should only be used when the target remains technically realistic.

---

# Risk Management

The Moving Average Rule does not remove risk.

Every trade must still follow proper capital management.

For this course:

**Total trading session risk must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

The trader should also calculate position size before entry.

---

# Example of a Bullish Setup

Market:

- 5-minute chart

EMA alignment:

- 21 EMA above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA above 200 EMA

Price action:

- price is above all three EMAs
- price retraces toward the 21 EMA
- bullish rejection appears
- market structure remains bullish

Possible action:

- consider a buy entry after confirmation
- place the stop-loss below the invalidation area
- target the next technical level
- seek approximately 1:3 when realistic

---

# Example of a Bearish Setup

Market:

- 5-minute chart

EMA alignment:

- 21 EMA below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA below 200 EMA

Price action:

- price is below all three EMAs
- price retraces toward the 50 EMA
- bearish rejection appears
- market structure remains bearish

Possible action:

- consider a sell entry after confirmation
- place the stop-loss above the invalidation area
- target the next technical level
- seek approximately 1:3 when realistic

---

# Signs the Trend May Be Weakening

A trend may be weakening when:

- the 21 EMA begins crossing the 50 EMA repeatedly
- the EMAs lose separation
- the slopes become flat
- price closes strongly through all three EMAs
- the 50 EMA begins crossing the 200 EMA
- market structure changes

When these signs appear, avoid forcing a trade.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- entering without correct EMA alignment
- trading against the EMA order
- entering on every EMA touch
- ignoring market structure
- chasing price far from the EMAs
- trading when the EMAs are flat
- entering without confirmation
- risking too much
- placing random stop-losses
- assuming the method guarantees profit

The method is a structured decision-making tool, not a guarantee.

---

# The Dessetra Moving Average Checklist

Before entering, confirm:

1. Am I using the 5-minute timeframe?
2. Is the 21 EMA red?
3. Is the 50 EMA yellow?
4. Is the 200 EMA blue?
5. Are the EMAs correctly ordered?
6. Are the EMAs pointing in the same direction?
7. Is there visible separation between them?
8. Has price retraced toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA?
9. Has valid confirmation appeared?
10. Does market structure support the entry?
11. Is the stop-loss technically valid?
12. Is the target realistic?
13. Does the trade support approximately 1:3?
14. Will total session risk remain below 5%?
15. Am I emotionally calm?

If important conditions are missing, wait.

---

# Key Principle

The Moving Average Rule does not encourage traders to chase price.

It teaches traders to:

- identify the dominant trend
- confirm correct EMA alignment
- wait for retracement
- enter only after confirmation
- manage risk professionally

The quality of the setup is more important than the number of trades taken.

# Lesson Summary

The Dessetra Moving Average Rule uses three EMAs:

- 21 EMA — red
- 50 EMA — yellow
- 200 EMA — blue

They should be displayed with increasing thickness.

For an uptrend:

- 21 EMA is above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA is above 200 EMA

For a downtrend:

- 21 EMA is below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA is below 200 EMA

The preferred entry occurs when price retraces toward one of the moving averages, especially the 21 EMA or 50 EMA, and then provides confirmation in the direction of the trend.

The method is primarily applied to 5-minute scalping.

For this course:

- traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately **1:3**
- total trading session risk must **never exceed 5%** of total trading capital

The Moving Average Rule works best when trend alignment, retracement, confirmation and risk management are all present together.
