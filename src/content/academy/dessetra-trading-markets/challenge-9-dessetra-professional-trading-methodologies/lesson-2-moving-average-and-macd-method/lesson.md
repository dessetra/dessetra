# Moving Average and MACD Method

In the previous lesson, you learned the Dessetra Moving Average Rule using the:

- 21 EMA
- 50 EMA
- 200 EMA

That strategy already provides a structured way to identify trends and possible retracement entries.

This lesson introduces another indicator that strengthens the decision-making process.

That indicator is the **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence).**

The MACD is **not used to replace the Moving Average Rule.**

Instead, it acts as a confirmation tool.

---

# Purpose of the MACD

The purpose of adding the MACD is to help confirm that momentum agrees with the direction shown by the moving averages.

Think of the Moving Average Rule as identifying the trend.

Think of the MACD as confirming whether momentum is supporting that trend.

When both indicators agree, confidence in the setup becomes stronger.

---

# MACD Configuration

For the Dessetra Trading Methodology, configure the MACD as follows:

- Fast Length: **03**
- Slow Length: **09**
- Signal Length: **16**

These settings should remain consistent whenever this methodology is applied.

---

# Understanding the MACD

The MACD consists of:

- MACD Line
- Signal Line
- Zero Line

For this methodology, the most important reference is the **Zero Line**.

The relationship between the MACD and the Zero Line helps determine whether buying or selling momentum is increasing.

---

# The Zero Line

The Zero Line separates bullish momentum from bearish momentum.

When momentum shifts above the Zero Line, buyers are becoming stronger.

When momentum shifts below the Zero Line, sellers are becoming stronger.

The Zero Line therefore becomes an important confirmation level.

---

# Buy Confirmation

A potential buy confirmation occurs when:

**The MACD Signal Line crosses upward through the Zero Line.**

This suggests that bullish momentum is beginning to dominate.

However, this alone is **not** a reason to buy.

It should only confirm what the Moving Average Rule is already showing.

---

# Sell Confirmation

A potential sell confirmation occurs when:

**The MACD Signal Line crosses downward through the Zero Line.**

This indicates that bearish momentum is becoming stronger.

Again, this should confirm an existing Moving Average setup rather than replace it.

---

# The First Rule

Always analyse the Moving Averages first.

Never begin your analysis with the MACD.

Ask:

- Are the EMAs properly aligned?
- Is there a clear trend?
- Has price retraced?

Only after answering these questions should the MACD be consulted.

---

# Bullish Conditions

For a bullish setup, confirm:

- 21 EMA above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA above 200 EMA
- Price trading above the EMAs
- Price retracing toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA
- MACD Signal Line crossing upward through the Zero Line

When these conditions occur together, the probability of a quality setup may improve.

---

# Bearish Conditions

For a bearish setup, confirm:

- 21 EMA below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA below 200 EMA
- Price trading below the EMAs
- Price retracing toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA
- MACD Signal Line crossing downward through the Zero Line

When all conditions agree, the trader may consider a sell opportunity.

---

# Why Use Both Indicators?

Each indicator provides different information.

The Moving Averages answer:

"Which direction is the market trending?"

The MACD answers:

"Is momentum supporting that trend?"

When both agree, they provide stronger confirmation than either indicator alone.

---

# Waiting for Retracement

Do not buy simply because the MACD crosses upward.

Do not sell simply because the MACD crosses downward.

Continue waiting for price to retrace toward:

- the 21 EMA
- or the 50 EMA

The retracement remains an important part of the Dessetra methodology.

---

# Importance of Confirmation

Confirmation helps avoid emotional trading.

A trader should never rush into a position because one indicator changes direction.

Instead, allow:

- EMA alignment
- retracement
- MACD confirmation

to work together.

---

# Example of a Buy Setup

Suppose the 5-minute chart shows:

- 21 EMA above 50 EMA
- 50 EMA above 200 EMA
- Price above all three EMAs

Price retraces toward the 21 EMA.

At the same time, the MACD Signal Line crosses upward through the Zero Line.

The trader now has:

- trend confirmation
- retracement
- momentum confirmation

A buy opportunity may now be considered after normal entry confirmation.

---

# Example of a Sell Setup

Suppose the market shows:

- 21 EMA below 50 EMA
- 50 EMA below 200 EMA
- Price below all three EMAs

Price retraces toward the 50 EMA.

The MACD Signal Line crosses downward through the Zero Line.

The trader now has:

- bearish trend
- retracement
- bearish momentum confirmation

A sell opportunity may now be considered.

---

# When to Ignore the MACD

Do not rely on MACD if:

- the Moving Averages are not aligned
- price is moving sideways
- the EMAs are flat
- the market is consolidating

The Moving Average Rule remains the primary decision-making tool.

---

# Avoid Trading Every MACD Cross

Many beginners trade every MACD crossover.

This methodology does **not** teach that.

A MACD signal without trend alignment can produce many poor trades.

Always begin with market structure.

---

# The Role of Risk Management

Even when every condition appears perfect, losses can still occur.

Every trade should continue to follow proper capital management.

For this course:

**Total trading session risk must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

---

# Position Sizing

Before entering:

- calculate position size
- identify the stop-loss
- identify the profit target

Never decide position size emotionally.

---

# Profit Targets

Profit targets should continue to follow market structure.

Whenever technically realistic, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

The market should determine the target—not emotion.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- trading MACD alone
- ignoring EMA alignment
- buying after extended price movement
- selling after price has fallen too far
- ignoring retracements
- trading every MACD crossover
- entering without confirmation
- risking more than planned

Remember that MACD is a confirmation tool, not a standalone trading system.

---

# Dessetra Moving Average + MACD Checklist

Before entering a trade, confirm:

1. Am I on the 5-minute timeframe?
2. Are the EMAs correctly aligned?
3. Is the market clearly trending?
4. Has price retraced toward the 21 EMA or 50 EMA?
5. Has the MACD Signal Line crossed the Zero Line in the trend direction?
6. Is the market structure still valid?
7. Is my stop-loss technically placed?
8. Is my target realistic?
9. Does the trade support approximately 1:3 risk-to-reward?
10. Will my total session risk remain below 5%?

Only when these questions are answered positively should a trade be considered.

---

# Key Principle

The MACD strengthens the Moving Average Rule.

It should never replace it.

The Moving Averages identify the trend.

The MACD confirms momentum.

Together they help traders identify higher-quality trading opportunities while maintaining discipline and proper risk management.
