# Trading Potential Support and Resistance Zones

One of the most reliable ways of understanding market behaviour is by identifying areas where price has repeatedly changed direction.

These areas are known as **Support** and **Resistance** zones.

Rather than treating them as exact prices, traders should think of them as **zones** where buyers or sellers are likely to become active.

The Dessetra methodology focuses on identifying these zones and waiting for price to revisit them before considering a trade.

---

# What is a Support Zone?

A support zone is an area where buyers are likely to enter the market.

When price reaches this zone, buying pressure may become stronger than selling pressure.

As a result, price may:

- Slow down
- Stop falling
- Reverse upward
- Continue the existing uptrend

Support should always be treated as a zone instead of a single price.

---

# What is a Resistance Zone?

A resistance zone is an area where sellers are likely to become active.

When price reaches this area, selling pressure may become stronger than buying pressure.

Price may therefore:

- Slow down
- Stop rising
- Reverse downward
- Continue the existing downtrend

Like support, resistance is also a zone rather than one exact price.

---

# Why Do These Zones Work?

Support and resistance exist because many traders notice the same important price areas.

When price returns to those areas, traders may:

- open new positions
- close existing positions
- take profits
- defend previous entries

This often creates noticeable reactions in the market.

---

# Think in Zones, Not Lines

Many beginners draw one horizontal line.

Professional traders normally identify an area instead.

Price rarely reverses at one exact price.

Instead, it usually reacts somewhere within a small range.

This is why we refer to them as support and resistance **zones**.

---

# Market Memory

Financial markets have memory.

If buyers previously defended a certain area, they may be interested again when price returns.

Likewise, if sellers previously rejected a price level, they may defend it again.

This repeated behaviour is what creates potential trading opportunities.

---

# Multiple Timeframe Analysis

The Dessetra methodology places strong emphasis on analysing more than one timeframe.

Higher timeframes provide stronger market structure.

Lower timeframes provide more precise entries.

Both should work together.

---

# Example Using the 4-Hour Chart

Suppose the market reaches an important support area on the 4-hour chart.

At first glance, it may appear that price touched the area only once.

However, when switching to the 15-minute chart, something different may be observed.

Price may have reacted inside that same zone several times before finally moving away.

This provides much more information about buyer activity.

---

# The Importance of Lower Timeframes

A higher timeframe compresses many smaller price movements into one candle.

Inside one 4-hour candle, dozens of 15-minute candles exist.

These smaller candles often reveal how buyers and sellers are actually fighting within the zone.

This helps traders understand the strength of the area.

---

# Repeated Reactions Increase Confidence

One reaction alone is usually not enough.

The Dessetra methodology prefers zones that have already shown repeated reactions.

For example:

- First visit
- Second visit
- Third visit

The more times a zone has produced meaningful reactions without breaking, the more attention it deserves.

---

# Trading the Second and Third Visit

One important principle of this methodology is patience.

Instead of trading the very first touch of a newly discovered zone, traders often look for the **second or third visit**.

Why?

Because the market has already demonstrated that buyers or sellers are interested in defending that area.

Repeated reactions increase confidence in the zone.

---

# Example of a Support Zone

Imagine price reaches an important support area.

During the first visit:

- buyers enter
- price rises

Several hours later price returns.

This is the second visit.

If buyers again defend the same area, the trader may begin looking for buying opportunities.

If price later returns for a third visit and buyers continue defending the zone, confidence in that area becomes even stronger.

---

# Example of a Resistance Zone

Price reaches an important resistance area.

On the first visit:

- sellers enter
- price falls

Later price returns.

Again sellers defend the same zone.

When price returns a third time and selling pressure appears again, the trader may consider another selling opportunity after confirmation.

---

# Combining Timeframes

A major advantage of this methodology is combining higher and lower timeframes.

For example:

Daily Chart

- identifies the major resistance zone

1-Hour Chart

- provides more detailed structure

15-Minute Chart

- identifies confirmation and precise entries

This creates a structured trading process.

---

# Another Example

Suppose a resistance zone is identified on the Daily chart.

Instead of immediately selling, the trader moves to the 1-hour chart.

On the 1-hour timeframe, price reaches that same area several times.

Each visit produces bearish rejection.

The trader now has:

- higher timeframe resistance
- repeated lower timeframe rejection
- stronger confidence

The entry can then be planned with greater precision.

---

# Confirmation is Still Required

Support and resistance zones are not automatic entry signals.

When price reaches a zone, traders should still wait for confirmation.

Possible confirmation includes:

- bullish rejection candle
- bearish rejection candle
- engulfing pattern
- break of minor market structure
- momentum returning in the expected direction

Confirmation reduces unnecessary risk.

---

# False Breakouts

Sometimes price moves beyond a support or resistance zone before reversing.

These movements are known as false breakouts.

False breakouts are common.

This is another reason why traders should avoid entering immediately when price first reaches a zone.

Waiting for confirmation often improves decision-making.

---

# Strong Zones

A strong support or resistance zone often has:

- multiple previous reactions
- clear reversals
- strong momentum away from the area
- repeated market respect
- higher timeframe significance

The stronger these characteristics become, the more attention the zone deserves.

---

# Weak Zones

Weak zones often display:

- only one reaction
- frequent price breaks
- sideways movement
- poor market structure
- little buying or selling pressure

Weak zones should be treated with caution.

---

# Avoid Guessing

Never assume every horizontal area will become support or resistance.

Allow the market to provide evidence first.

Professional traders react to market behaviour.

They do not predict without evidence.

---

# Risk Management

Even the strongest support or resistance zone can fail.

Every trade should therefore include:

- planned stop-loss
- calculated position size
- realistic target

For this course:

**Total trading session risk must never exceed 5% of total trading capital.**

---

# Profit Targets

Profit targets should be based on market structure.

Whenever technically realistic, traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately:

**1:3**

Do not force unrealistic targets simply to achieve the ratio.

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

- treating zones as exact prices
- trading the first touch without confirmation
- ignoring higher timeframes
- ignoring repeated reactions
- forcing trades
- entering without confirmation
- risking too much capital
- moving stop-losses emotionally

---

# Dessetra Support & Resistance Checklist

Before entering a trade, ask:

1. Is this a genuine support or resistance zone?
2. Has price reacted here before?
3. Is this the second or third visit?
4. Does the higher timeframe support this zone?
5. Has the lower timeframe confirmed the reaction?
6. Has a rejection candle appeared?
7. Is market structure still valid?
8. Is my stop-loss technically placed?
9. Is my target realistic?
10. Does the trade support approximately 1:3?
11. Will total session risk remain below 5%?

If important conditions are missing, continue waiting.

---

# Key Principle

The Dessetra methodology does not trade every support or resistance level.

Instead, it identifies important zones, studies repeated market reactions across multiple timeframes, and waits patiently for confirmation before entering.

Patience creates better trading decisions.

---

# Lesson Summary

Support zones are areas where buyers are likely to enter the market.

Resistance zones are areas where sellers are likely to become active.

Rather than trading the very first touch, the Dessetra methodology often looks for the **second and third visit** after the market has already demonstrated respect for that zone.

Higher timeframes identify the important zones.

Lower timeframes provide detailed confirmation and precise entries.

For this course:

- traders should generally seek an average risk-to-reward ratio of approximately **1:3**
- total trading session risk must **never exceed 5%** of total trading capital

Professional traders do not predict reactions at support and resistance.

They wait for the market to confirm that buyers or sellers are actively defending the zone before making a trading decision.
