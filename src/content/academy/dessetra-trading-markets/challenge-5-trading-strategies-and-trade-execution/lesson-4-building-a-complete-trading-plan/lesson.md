## What Does Independent Mean?

Independent does not mean:

> "I know where price will go."

It means you can independently answer:

- What is the current market condition?
- What is the higher-timeframe direction?
- Where are the important zones?
- Does my strategy apply here?
- Is confirmation present?
- Where is my entry?
- Where is my invalidation?
- How much can I risk?
- What position size should I use?
- Where is my target?
- Does the R:R make sense?
- What would cancel this trade?
- What will I do if the trade loses?
- How will I review the result?

The objective is not prediction.

The objective is:

**Structured decision-making under uncertainty.**

## Stage 1 â€” Prepare

Before analysing entries, prepare your environment.

Check:

- trading platform
- internet connection
- economic calendar
- markets you are permitted to trade
- trading session
- current account balance/equity
- existing positions
- existing risk
- mental and emotional condition

Then ask:

**What would make me stop trading today?**

Possible stopping conditions include:

- session-risk ceiling reached
- three consecutive losses
- emotional instability
- technical/platform problems
- unsuitable market conditions

Preparation should happen **before** the first opportunity appears.

## Session Risk

The Dessetra risk rule remains:

**Maximum combined session risk = 5% of trading capital**

This is the maximum ceiling.

It is **not** a requirement to risk 5%.

If existing positions already consume your allowed session exposure:

**Do not add another trade.**

---

## Risk-to-Reward

Your target should come from logical market structure.

Dessetra generally seeks approximately:

**1:3 R:R when structure realistically supports it.**

But:

**Do not force 1:3.**

If important Resistance, Support or another obstacle makes the target unrealistic, reconsider the trade.

Sometimes the correct decision is:

**NO TRADE**

## Stage 5 â€” Execute

If the setup qualifies and risk is acceptable:

Execute according to your written strategy.

After entry:

- respect the Stop Loss
- follow the predefined management rule
- do not widen the Stop Loss emotionally
- do not randomly change Take Profit
- do not increase risk
- do not chase another position
- do not manage from fear or greed

Your work after entry should become simpler because most decisions were already made before entry.

## Separate Outcome From Process

A trade has two different dimensions:

**Outcome**

and:

**Execution Quality**

Consider four possibilities.

### Good Process + Win

Rules followed and trade won.

### Good Process + Loss

Rules followed and trade lost.

This is a normal trading outcome.

### Bad Process + Win

Rules were broken but the trade happened to win.

Do not reward the mistake.

### Bad Process + Loss

Rules were broken and the trade lost.

The execution problem must be addressed.

This distinction is essential.

A profitable mistake is still a mistake.

## Daily Review

At the end of a trading session, review:

- number of trades
- total risk
- result
- valid setups
- rejected setups
- rule violations
- emotional behaviour
- execution quality
- lessons learned

The review does not need to be long.

It needs to be consistent.

---

## Weekly Review

At the end of a larger group of trades, examine:

- total trades
- win rate
- average win
- average loss
- net R
- expectancy
- largest drawdown
- longest losing streak
- rule compliance
- repeated mistakes
- market conditions
- session performance

Look for **patterns**.

Do not overreact to one trade.

## Your Strategy Can Change

A strategy is not permanently frozen.

Markets and your understanding can change.

But strategy changes should be based on:

**Evidence**

not:

**Emotion**

Do not change a rule:

- during an open trade
- immediately after one loss
- because you are frustrated
- because you missed a trade
- because another trader uses different rules
- simply to make an old losing trade look avoidable

---

## Use Strategy Versions

When evidence justifies an adjustment, create a new version.

For example:

**Strategy 1.0**

Then:

**Strategy 1.1**

Record:

- date
- rule changed
- reason
- evidence
- expected effect

Then test the new version.

This prevents your strategy from slowly changing without you realizing it.

---

## Change One Important Variable at a Time

Suppose your backtesting and demo journal repeatedly show that one confirmation rule creates poor entries.

Do not simultaneously change:

- EMA
- MACD
- timeframe
- Stop Loss
- target
- entry trigger

Instead:

1. Identify the repeated problem.
2. Propose one meaningful adjustment.
3. Test it.
4. Compare results.
5. Keep or reject the change.

This makes improvement measurable.

---

## Responding to Drawdown

Every strategy can experience losing periods.

When drawdown occurs, the solution is not:

**Increase risk to recover faster.**

Instead:

- reduce exposure if your plan requires it
- review recent trades
- identify execution errors
- examine whether market conditions changed
- return to backtesting if necessary
- return to demo testing if necessary

The objective is to restore a reliable process.

Not to recover money emotionally.

---

## When Should You Pause?

Pause trading when:

- you repeatedly break your rules
- emotional decisions are increasing
- drawdown exceeds your planned threshold
- strategy behaviour differs significantly from testing
- you no longer understand the market condition
- technical/platform problems affect execution
- your journal shows repeated uncontrolled mistakes

Stopping can be part of professional risk management.

---

## Returning to Demo Is Not Failure

Suppose you eventually begin trading with real capital and discover:

- fear is changing your exits
- risk is becoming inconsistent
- you are revenge trading
- your strategy is behaving differently
- you have stopped journaling

Returning to demo can help you rebuild execution without continuing to expose capital.

The goal is not to prove that you are always ready.

The goal is to recognize when your process needs work.

---

## Demo Results Do Not Guarantee Live Results

Backtesting and demo trading are valuable.

But they have limitations.

Live trading can introduce:

- real financial pressure
- stronger emotional reactions
- different execution conditions
- slippage
- changing spreads
- changing market environments

Therefore:

**Historical or demo profitability does not guarantee future live profitability.**

---

## Are You Ready for Live Trading?

There is no single quiz score or number of demo trades that can guarantee readiness.

Instead, examine your evidence.

Ask:

### Strategy

- Are my rules clearly written?
- Can I identify my setup consistently?
- Have I tested the strategy across a meaningful sample?

### Risk

- Do I calculate position size correctly?
- Do I consistently respect Stop Loss?
- Do I remain within my risk limits?

### Execution

- Do I wait for confirmation?
- Can I accept no-trade sessions?
- Do I avoid chasing entries?

### Psychology

- Can I accept losses without revenge trading?
- Can I maintain the same risk after wins and losses?
- Can I follow the plan under pressure?

### Review

- Do I journal consistently?
- Do I review my results?
- Are strategy changes based on evidence?

If important answers are:

**NO**

continue developing them before taking greater financial risk.

---

## If You Decide to Trade Live

The decision to risk real capital is yours.

If you independently decide to move beyond demo:

**Start small.**

Do not assume that because a strategy performed well during testing you should immediately use large positions.

Use capital you can afford to lose.

Continue:

- position sizing
- Stop Loss discipline
- journaling
- screenshots
- process scoring
- daily review
- strategy evaluation

The risk rules do not disappear when real money begins.

They become even more important.

---

## Do Not Chase Income Targets

Avoid thinking:

> "I must make $100 today."

or:

> "I need 10% this week."

The market does not have to provide a valid setup simply because you have a financial target.

A forced target can encourage:

- overtrading
- excessive risk
- weak setups
- revenge trading
- emotional decisions

Focus on executing valid opportunities.

---

## A No-Trade Day Can Be Successful

Suppose you analyse the market for an entire session.

No setup satisfies your strategy.

You take:

**0 trades**

Did you fail?

No.

If your strategy said:

**Do not trade**

and you followed it, your execution was correct.

Discipline is demonstrated by both:

**Taking valid trades**

and:

**Rejecting invalid ones.**

## Final Key Takeaways

- Independent trading means making structured decisions using your own defined process.
- Preparation comes before execution.
- Analyse from context toward entry.
- A setup must qualify before risk is considered.
- Risk must be calculated before position size.
- The 5% maximum session-risk ceiling remains in force.
- Approximately 1:3 R:R should only be sought when structure supports it.
- Follow predefined management rules.
- Separate trade outcome from execution quality.
- Journal every trade.
- Review groups of trades for patterns.
- Change strategy rules from evidence, not emotion.
- Use strategy versions when making meaningful changes.
- Reduce or pause risk when your process deteriorates.
- Returning to demo is a valid development tool.
- Backtest and demo results do not guarantee future profits.
- Starting live trading is an individual risk decision, not an automatic result of completing the course.
- A no-trade decision can demonstrate excellent discipline.
- Consistency comes from repeatedly following a measurable process.
