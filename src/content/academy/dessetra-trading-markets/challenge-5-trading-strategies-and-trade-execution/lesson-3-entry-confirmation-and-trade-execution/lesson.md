# Live-Market Demo Challenge

You have built your strategy.

You have backtested it.

Now comes a different test:

**Can you follow those same rules while the market is moving in real time?**

Historical testing allows you to study past price action.

Demo forward testing removes one important advantage:

**You do not know what the next candle will do.**

This makes the exercise much closer to actual trading decision-making.

---

## What Is Forward Testing?

Forward testing means applying your strategy to current market conditions as they develop.

Instead of going backward through historical charts, you:

1. Analyse the current market.
2. Wait for your strategy conditions.
3. Execute only when the setup qualifies.
4. Manage the trade according to your rules.
5. Record the result.
6. Review your execution.

For this Challenge, all forward testing should be completed using a:

**DEMO ACCOUNT**

The objective is not to make real money.

The objective is to prove that you can execute your process consistently.

---

## Backtesting vs Forward Testing

## Backtesting

You apply your rules to:

**Historical price action**

It helps evaluate:

- whether setups occurred
- historical win/loss behaviour
- R:R
- expectancy
- drawdown
- losing streaks
- clarity of strategy rules

## Forward Testing

You apply your rules to:

**Current market conditions**

It helps evaluate:

- patience
- real-time analysis
- execution
- spread
- possible slippage
- emotional response
- rule compliance
- practical usability

A strategy that looks easy to execute historically may feel very different when you must wait for the setup in real time.

---

## Demo Does Not Mean Random

Because demo money is not real money, some traders stop taking the exercise seriously.

They may:

- use unrealistic lot sizes
- enter without confirmation
- take dozens of trades
- ignore Stop Losses
- constantly change strategy
- trade because they are bored

That produces poor evidence.

Treat the demo account as though the capital matters.

Use the same:

**Strategy â†’ Risk Rules â†’ Execution Rules â†’ Journal**

that you would expect yourself to follow later.

## Your Strategy Is Now Frozen

For this Challenge, use the strategy you developed and reviewed during Lessons 1 and 2.

Do not change the strategy because:

- one trade loses
- you miss an entry
- another indicator looks interesting
- another trader has a different opinion
- price moves without you
- you become impatient

You are testing your ability to execute a defined process.

If you discover a weakness, record it.

Do not rewrite the rules during an open trade.

## Before Every Trading Session

Do not immediately search for entries.

Start with preparation.

Check:

1. Is MT5 functioning correctly?
2. Is your internet connection stable?
3. Which instrument are you trading?
4. Are important scheduled economic events approaching?
5. What is the higher-timeframe direction?
6. What important levels or zones are visible?
7. What market condition is present?
8. Does that condition suit your strategy?
9. What is your maximum session risk?
10. How much risk is already open?
11. Are you mentally prepared to trade?
12. What conditions will make you stop?

This turns market preparation into a routine.

---

## Wait for Your Setup

Your job is not to find a trade every day.

Your job is to identify when your strategy conditions actually exist.

Remember:

**Trend â†’ Structure â†’ Zone â†’ EMA â†’ Momentum â†’ Entry Trigger**

If your strategy requires these conditions and only four are present:

**WAIT.**

If the setup becomes invalid:

**REJECT.**

Do not lower your standards simply because you have spent a long time watching the chart.

---

## No Setup = No Trade

Some sessions will produce no valid opportunity.

That is normal.

If nothing qualifies, record:

**NO TRADE**

A no-trade session can demonstrate excellent discipline.

You are not being evaluated on how frequently you click Buy or Sell.

You are evaluating whether you can obey the strategy.

## Do Not Interfere Emotionally

Once the trade is open, follow your management rules.

Do not:

- widen the Stop Loss
- remove the Stop Loss
- close because of fear without a strategy-based reason
- move to break-even randomly
- increase the position to recover another loss
- change the Take Profit because of greed

If your strategy uses:

**Fixed SL and TP**

then follow that.

If your tested strategy uses:

**Break-even**

follow the predefined break-even rule.

If it uses:

**Structure-based management**

follow the structure rule.

The important word is:

**Predefined.**

## What If the Trade Loses?

First:

**Accept the planned result.**

Then ask:

### Did I follow the strategy?

If yes:

Record it as a normal strategy loss.

### Did I violate the strategy?

If yes:

Record the specific execution error.

Do not immediately open another trade to recover the loss.

---

## Three Consecutive Losses

Our Challenge 4 behavioural safeguard remains active:

**Three consecutive losses = stop trading for the day.**

If this happens:

1. Record the third trade.
2. Close the trading platform.
3. Review all three setups.
4. Check whether the strategy rules were followed.
5. Continue on another trading day.

Do not increase risk to recover.

---

## Maximum Session Risk

The Dessetra rule also remains:

**Maximum combined session risk = 5% of trading capital**

This is a ceiling.

If your existing/open risk reaches the maximum permitted session exposure:

**No additional trade should be opened.**

The three-loss safeguard and session-risk ceiling are independent rules.

Whichever stopping condition is reached first should be respected.

---

## What If You Miss the Entry?

Suppose you were waiting for a valid Buy.

The trigger occurred while you were away.

When you return, price has already moved strongly.

Do not chase.

Recalculate:

- current entry
- invalidation
- Stop Loss
- target
- R:R

If the original opportunity no longer exists:

**RECORD: MISSED TRADE**

Then wait for a new setup.

Missing a trade is not a strategy failure.

Breaking your rules because you missed it is an execution failure.

## Compare Backtest and Demo Behaviour

After accumulating demo trades, compare them with your historical test.

Ask:

- Are setups appearing as expected?
- Am I entering according to the same rules?
- Am I hesitating?
- Am I entering too early?
- Am I chasing?
- Are spreads affecting entries?
- Is execution practical?
- Am I managing trades differently because of emotion?
- Are current results very different from the historical sample?

Do not expect the numbers to match exactly.

The objective is to identify meaningful differences.

## Execution Quality Matters

Suppose two learners complete ten demo trades.

### Trader A

6 wins  
4 losses

But frequently:

- entered early
- exceeded risk
- ignored confirmation

### Trader B

4 wins  
6 losses

But:

- followed every entry rule
- respected position sizing
- respected SL
- journaled every trade
- made no emotional entries

Trader B may currently demonstrate **better process discipline**, even though the short-term financial result is weaker.

A small sample of profits does not excuse poor execution.

## Do Not Rush Toward Live Money

Completing ten demo trades does **not** automatically mean:

**You are ready to risk real money.**

Ten trades are an educational exercise, not proof of long-term performance.

Your evidence may instead tell you:

- continue demo testing
- improve execution
- collect a larger sample
- clarify a strategy rule
- return to backtesting
- work on psychological discipline

That is a successful outcome if it prevents premature risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Forward testing applies your strategy to current market conditions.
- Use a demo account for this Challenge.
- Demo trading should not become random trading.
- Keep your strategy rules stable during the test.
- Prepare before searching for entries.
- Start analysis from the higher timeframe.
- No valid setup means no trade.
- Calculate risk and position size before execution.
- Do not manage trades emotionally.
- Record both financial results and emotional behaviour.
- Respect the three-consecutive-loss safeguard.
- Never exceed the 5% maximum combined session-risk ceiling.
- Do not chase missed trades.
- Record rejected setups as evidence of discipline.
- Compare demo execution with your historical backtest.
- Evaluate process quality as well as profit or loss.
- Ten demo trades are a learning exercise, not proof of future profitability.
