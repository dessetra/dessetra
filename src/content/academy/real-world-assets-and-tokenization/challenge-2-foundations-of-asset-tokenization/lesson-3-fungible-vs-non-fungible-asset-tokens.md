# Fungible vs Non-Fungible Asset Tokens

Not all blockchain tokens represent assets in the same way. Some assets are divided into identical units that can be exchanged freely, while others represent unique items that cannot be replaced by another token.

Understanding the difference between fungible and non-fungible tokens is essential when designing or evaluating a Real-World Asset (RWA) project.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define fungible and non-fungible tokens.
- Distinguish between their characteristics.
- Identify suitable use cases for each.
- Understand why token type matters in RWA projects.

---

## 1. What Is a Fungible Token?

A fungible token is interchangeable with another token of the same type and value.

For example:

- One token is equal to another identical token.
- It does not matter which specific token you own.
- Every token carries the same rights.

Examples include:

- Cryptocurrencies
- Stablecoins
- Fractional ownership tokens
- Tokenized investment funds
- Tokenized bonds

Fungible tokens are commonly used when an asset is divided into equal portions.

---

## 2. What Is a Non-Fungible Token?

A non-fungible token (NFT) represents something unique.

Each token has its own identity and cannot simply be exchanged for another token on a one-to-one basis.

Examples include:

- A specific piece of artwork
- A unique property title
- A rare collectible
- A patent
- A luxury watch
- A particular parcel of land

Each NFT may carry different characteristics or ownership rights.

---

## 3. Comparing Both Types

### Fungible Tokens

Characteristics:

- Identical value.
- Easily interchangeable.
- Can be divided into smaller units (depending on the token standard).
- Suitable for shared ownership.

Examples:

- Tokenized gold.
- Fractional real estate investments.
- Tokenized government bonds.

---

### Non-Fungible Tokens

Characteristics:

- Unique identity.
- Not interchangeable.
- Represent individual assets.
- Each token may have different characteristics.

Examples:

- Individual property deeds.
- Rare artwork.
- Luxury collectibles.
- Intellectual property rights.

---

## 4. Which One Is Used in RWA?

Both token types are used depending on the asset.

Examples:

### Real Estate Investment Platform

A commercial building may be divided into thousands of identical ownership tokens.

This is generally a fungible structure.

---

### Property Ownership Certificate

A token representing ownership of one specific house with its own legal records is generally non-fungible.

---

### Gold Investment

If one kilogram of gold is divided into equal ownership units, the tokens are typically fungible.

---

### Fine Art

Each painting is unique, making non-fungible tokens more appropriate.

---

## 5. Choosing the Right Structure

Before issuing tokens, project developers should consider:

- Is the asset unique?
- Will ownership be shared?
- Will every investor receive identical rights?
- Will the tokens be traded individually?
- What legal structure supports the project?

The answers help determine whether fungible or non-fungible tokens are more appropriate.

---

## Practical Example

A company owns a luxury hotel.

### Option A

The hotel is divided into **500,000 equal investment tokens**.

Each token provides the same economic rights.

This is a **fungible token model**.

---

### Option B

Each hotel room receives its own individual ownership token.

Each room has different characteristics, value and ownership records.

This is a **non-fungible token model**.

Both approaches can be valid depending on the project's objectives and legal structure.

## Key Takeaways

- Fungible tokens are interchangeable and usually represent equal ownership rights.
- Non-fungible tokens represent unique assets or rights.
- The choice depends on the nature of the underlying asset.
- Both token types are important within the RWA ecosystem.
- Legal structure and project objectives determine the most appropriate token model.
