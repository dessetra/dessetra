# How Physical Assets Become Digital Tokens

Tokenization is much more than creating a digital token on a blockchain. It is a structured process that combines legal, financial and technical steps to connect a real-world asset with its digital representation.

Each stage is important because the value of the token depends on the legitimacy and proper management of the underlying asset.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain the stages of asset tokenization.
- Understand the legal and technical requirements.
- Identify the parties involved.
- Recognize why verification is essential.

---

## 1. Selecting the Asset

The process begins by identifying the asset that will be tokenized.

Examples include:

- A commercial building
- Agricultural land
- Gold reserves
- Government bonds
- Company shares
- Equipment
- Music royalties

The asset should have clear ownership and measurable value.

---

## 2. Verifying Ownership

Before tokenization begins, the ownership of the asset must be verified.

This may involve reviewing:

- Property titles
- Registration certificates
- Purchase agreements
- Financial statements
- Insurance records
- Legal contracts

Without proper verification, the token may not accurately represent the asset.

---

## 3. Valuing the Asset

An independent valuation may be carried out to determine the asset's current value.

The valuation helps establish:

- The total project value.
- The number of tokens to issue.
- The approximate value represented by each token.

Accurate valuation promotes fairness and transparency.

---

## 4. Creating the Legal Structure

The legal framework defines:

- Who owns the asset.
- What rights token holders receive.
- How income is distributed.
- How ownership transfers occur.
- What happens if disputes arise.

This is one of the most important stages of tokenization.

---

## 5. Issuing the Tokens

Once the legal structure is complete, digital tokens are created on a blockchain.

Each token represents the rights defined in the project's documentation.

The blockchain records:

- Token ownership
- Token transfers
- Transaction history
- Smart contract activity

The blockchain records the digital representation—it does not physically store the asset.

---

## 6. Managing the Asset

After tokenization, the asset still requires management.

Examples include:

- Property maintenance.
- Insurance renewals.
- Financial reporting.
- Rental collection.
- Compliance monitoring.
- Regulatory reporting.

The blockchain supports record keeping, but real-world management continues.

---

## 7. Ongoing Monitoring

Successful projects regularly monitor:

- Asset condition.
- Financial performance.
- Legal compliance.
- Market value.
- Investor reporting.

Keeping accurate records helps maintain confidence in the project.

---

## Practical Example

Imagine a company owns farmland valued at **$1 million**.

The company:

1. Verifies legal ownership.
2. Obtains an independent valuation.
3. Creates a legal structure for token holders.
4. Issues **100,000 blockchain tokens**.
5. Records token ownership on the blockchain.
6. Continues managing the farmland while providing reports to token holders.

Each token represents rights defined in the project's legal agreements rather than ownership of a specific piece of land.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Tokenization involves legal, financial and technical processes.
- Ownership verification is essential before issuing tokens.
- Asset valuation helps determine token distribution.
- Legal agreements define the rights of token holders.
- Real-world assets continue to require management after tokenization.
