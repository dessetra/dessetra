# Liquidity, Default and Counterparty Risk

Even when a Real-World Asset (RWA) project has a genuine asset, strong legal documentation and reliable blockchain technology, investors still face important financial risks. Three of the most significant are **liquidity risk**, **default risk** and **counterparty risk**.

Understanding these risks helps investors make informed decisions and evaluate whether a project matches their financial goals and risk tolerance.

## 1. Liquidity Risk

Liquidity refers to how quickly an asset can be bought or sold without significantly affecting its price.

A liquid investment usually has many buyers and sellers.

An illiquid investment may take a long time to sell or may require accepting a lower price.

Examples of assets that may experience lower liquidity include:

- Commercial real estate.
- Private company shares.
- Fine art.
- Collectibles.
- Specialized equipment.

Tokenization may improve accessibility, but it does not guarantee active trading.

---

## 2. Why Liquidity Matters

Liquidity affects an investor's ability to:

- Access cash quickly.
- Adjust investment strategies.
- Respond to changing market conditions.
- Reduce financial pressure during emergencies.

Before investing, consider:

- Is there an active marketplace?
- Are token transfers restricted?
- Is there sufficient trading activity?

---

## 3. Default Risk

Default risk is the possibility that a borrower or project fails to meet its financial obligations.

Examples include:

- Missing interest payments.
- Failing to repay a loan.
- Not distributing agreed rental income.
- Breaking contractual obligations.

Default risk is especially important for:

- Tokenized loans.
- Bonds.
- Invoice financing.
- Revenue-sharing agreements.

---

## 4. Counterparty Risk

Counterparty risk is the possibility that another party involved in the project fails to perform its responsibilities.

Examples include:

- Property managers.
- Custodians.
- Technology providers.
- Payment processors.
- Legal service providers.
- Asset issuers.

Even if the blockchain operates correctly, poor performance by one of these parties may negatively affect the project.

---

## 5. Managing Risk

Professional RWA projects reduce risk through:

- Independent audits.
- Qualified custodians.
- Legal agreements.
- Insurance.
- Strong governance.
- Transparent reporting.
- Financial reserves.
- Regular compliance reviews.

Investors should also diversify their investments rather than relying on a single project.

---

## 6. Questions Investors Should Ask

Before investing, consider asking:

- How easily can the tokens be sold?
- What happens if the borrower cannot repay?
- Who manages the underlying asset?
- Who safeguards the asset?
- Has the project been independently audited?
- Are financial reports available?
- What legal protections exist?

Clear answers to these questions improve confidence in the investment.

---

## Practical Example

An investor purchases tokens backed by a commercial office building.

Several risks exist:

- Few buyers are interested in purchasing the tokens, creating **liquidity risk**.
- A major tenant stops paying rent, reducing income available for distribution and increasing **default risk**.
- The property management company performs poorly, affecting maintenance and rental occupancy, creating **counterparty risk**.

Although the blockchain continues recording transactions correctly, these real-world events still influence the investment's performance.

## Key Takeaways

- Liquidity risk affects how easily an investment can be bought or sold.
- Default risk involves failure to meet financial obligations.
- Counterparty risk arises when another organization fails to perform its responsibilities.
- Strong governance, audits and transparency help reduce risk.
- Investors should evaluate all three risks before participating in any RWA project.
