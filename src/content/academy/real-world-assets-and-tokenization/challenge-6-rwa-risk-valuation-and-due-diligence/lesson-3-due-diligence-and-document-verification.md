# Due Diligence and Document Verification

Before investing in or launching a Real-World Asset (RWA) project, it is important to perform **due diligence**. Due diligence is the process of carefully reviewing an asset, its documentation, the people managing it and the legal structure behind it.

No matter how attractive an investment opportunity appears, investors should never rely solely on marketing materials. Proper verification helps identify potential risks before committing money or resources.

## 1. What Is Due Diligence?

Due diligence is a careful investigation carried out before making an investment or business decision.

Its purpose is to answer questions such as:

- Does the asset actually exist?
- Who legally owns it?
- Is its value reasonable?
- Are the legal documents valid?
- Are the people managing the project trustworthy?
- Does the project comply with applicable regulations?

Good due diligence helps investors make informed decisions.

---

## 2. Why Due Diligence Matters

Skipping due diligence may expose investors to:

- Fraud.
- Fake ownership claims.
- Overvalued assets.
- Poor management.
- Legal disputes.
- Financial losses.

Careful verification reduces these risks.

---

## 3. Documents Commonly Reviewed

Depending on the asset, important documents may include:

- Property title deeds.
- Purchase agreements.
- Valuation reports.
- Financial statements.
- Insurance policies.
- Audit reports.
- Tax records.
- Business registrations.
- Shareholder agreements.
- Intellectual property registrations.

Different projects require different documentation.

---

## 4. Verifying the Asset

Investors should confirm that:

- The asset exists.
- Ownership records are accurate.
- The asset matches the project description.
- Independent valuations are available.
- The asset is not subject to undisclosed legal disputes.

Verification should come from reliable and independent sources whenever possible.

---

## 5. Reviewing the Project Team

The quality of the management team is also important.

Consider:

- Experience.
- Professional qualifications.
- Business reputation.
- Previous projects.
- Governance structure.
- Transparency.

Strong leadership increases confidence in long-term project success.

---

## 6. Warning Signs

Some situations require additional investigation.

Examples include:

- Missing legal documents.
- Unrealistic profit promises.
- Refusal to answer investor questions.
- Unverified asset ownership.
- No independent audits.
- Poor transparency.
- Pressure to invest immediately.

These warning signs do not automatically mean a project is fraudulent, but they should encourage careful review.

---

## 7. Building a Due Diligence Checklist

Many professional investors use a checklist before making investment decisions.

Typical checklist items include:

- Asset verified.
- Ownership confirmed.
- Valuation completed.
- Legal documents reviewed.
- Compliance confirmed.
- Management evaluated.
- Custody arrangements verified.
- Risks identified.
- Exit strategy understood.

A checklist promotes consistency and reduces the chance of overlooking important details.

---

## Practical Example

An investor is considering purchasing tokens backed by agricultural land.

Before investing, the investor reviews:

- The land title.
- Independent valuation reports.
- Insurance documents.
- Government registration records.
- The experience of the management team.
- Annual audit reports.
- Compliance documentation.

Only after completing this review does the investor decide whether to participate.

## Key Takeaways

- Due diligence is a critical step before investing in any RWA project.
- Independent verification helps reduce fraud and investment risk.
- Legal documents, valuations and management quality should all be reviewed.
- Warning signs should never be ignored.
- A structured due diligence checklist supports better investment decisions.
