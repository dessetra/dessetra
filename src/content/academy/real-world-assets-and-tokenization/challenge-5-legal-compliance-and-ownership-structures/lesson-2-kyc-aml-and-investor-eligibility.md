# KYC, AML and Investor Eligibility

For Real-World Asset (RWA) projects to operate legally and responsibly, they must comply with financial regulations designed to prevent fraud, money laundering and other financial crimes.

Two of the most important compliance requirements are **Know Your Customer (KYC)** and **Anti-Money Laundering (AML)**. These procedures help protect investors, businesses and the integrity of the financial system.

---

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

## 1. What Is KYC?

**Know Your Customer (KYC)** is the process of verifying the identity of a customer before allowing them to use financial services or invest in regulated products.

The goal is to confirm that participants are who they claim to be.

Typical KYC information may include:

- Full legal name.
- Date of birth.
- Residential address.
- Government-issued identification.
- Selfie or facial verification.
- Proof of address.
- Contact information.

Different jurisdictions may require different verification procedures.

---

## 2. What Is AML?

**Anti-Money Laundering (AML)** refers to laws, regulations and procedures designed to prevent criminals from hiding the origin of illegally obtained money.

AML programs help organizations identify suspicious financial activities and reduce financial crime.

Examples include:

- Monitoring unusual transactions.
- Reporting suspicious activities where required.
- Verifying customer identities.
- Screening against sanctions or restricted lists.
- Keeping compliance records.

---

## 3. Why KYC and AML Matter

RWA projects often involve valuable assets and financial transactions.

Without proper compliance, projects may face:

- Fraud.
- Identity theft.
- Money laundering.
- Terrorist financing.
- Regulatory penalties.
- Loss of investor confidence.

Strong compliance helps build trust and supports long-term sustainability.

---

## 4. Investor Eligibility

Not every investment opportunity is available to every person.

Some projects may have eligibility requirements based on:

- Country of residence.
- Age.
- Regulatory restrictions.
- Completion of KYC verification.
- Applicable investment rules.

Eligibility requirements vary depending on the laws governing the project.

---

## 5. Ongoing Compliance

Compliance is not a one-time event.

Projects may periodically:

- Update customer information.
- Review identification documents.
- Monitor transaction activity.
- Reassess investor eligibility.
- Respond to changes in regulations.

Maintaining accurate records helps projects remain compliant over time.

---

## 6. Protecting Personal Information

KYC processes require sensitive personal information.

Professional organizations should:

- Store information securely.
- Limit access to authorized personnel.
- Follow applicable privacy laws.
- Protect customer data against unauthorized access.

Investors should also verify that a platform has appropriate security measures before submitting personal information.

---

## Practical Example

A company launches a tokenized real estate investment platform.

Before purchasing tokens, each investor must:

1. Create an account.
2. Submit a government-issued ID.
3. Complete identity verification.
4. Provide proof of address.
5. Pass compliance screening.

Only after successful verification can the investor purchase tokens.

This helps the project comply with applicable regulations while protecting all participants.
