# Special Purpose Vehicles and Asset Custody

Most Real-World Asset (RWA) projects involve valuable assets such as buildings, commodities, intellectual property or financial instruments. To protect these assets and clearly define investor rights, many projects use legal structures known as **Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs)** together with professional **asset custodians**.

These structures improve transparency, reduce operational risk and help projects comply with applicable laws.

## 1. What Is a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)?

A **Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)** is a separate legal entity created for a specific business purpose.

In RWA projects, an SPV is often established to own or control the underlying asset instead of an individual or operating company owning it directly.

This helps separate the asset from the company's other business activities and liabilities.

---

## 2. Why Use an SPV?

An SPV may provide several advantages:

- Clearly identifies who owns the asset.
- Separates the asset from other company operations.
- Simplifies investor participation.
- Supports legal and regulatory compliance.
- Improves transparency.
- Makes asset management easier.

Many institutional RWA projects use SPVs because they provide a structured legal framework.

---

## 3. What Is Asset Custody?

Asset custody refers to the secure protection of assets or important legal documents by a trusted organization or qualified custodian.

The custodian helps ensure that the underlying asset remains protected throughout the life of the project.

Examples include:

- Property title documents.
- Gold stored in secure vaults.
- Company share certificates.
- Legal contracts.
- Private cryptographic keys.
- Insurance records.

---

## 4. Responsibilities of a Custodian

A professional custodian may:

- Safeguard the asset.
- Maintain accurate records.
- Protect legal documentation.
- Verify asset availability.
- Support audits.
- Report asset status when required.

Custodians generally do not own the asset—they protect it on behalf of the project or its investors.

---

## 5. How SPVs and Custodians Work Together

A typical RWA project may operate as follows:

1. An SPV legally owns the asset.
2. A professional custodian safeguards the asset or related documentation.
3. Blockchain records token ownership.
4. Smart contracts automate predefined processes.
5. Investors hold blockchain tokens representing rights defined by legal agreements.

Each participant has a separate responsibility, helping create a more reliable system.

---

## 6. Risks of Poor Custody

Weak custody arrangements may lead to:

- Loss of important documents.
- Theft of physical assets.
- Unauthorized transfers.
- Fraud.
- Inaccurate records.
- Reduced investor confidence.

Choosing experienced custodians and maintaining strong internal controls can significantly reduce these risks.

---

## 7. Importance of Independent Audits

Many professional RWA projects undergo regular independent audits.

Audits help confirm:

- The asset exists.
- Ownership records are accurate.
- Custody procedures are followed.
- Financial reports are reliable.
- Legal documentation remains current.

Independent audits increase transparency and strengthen investor confidence.

---

## Practical Example

A company wishes to tokenize a commercial warehouse.

Instead of owning the warehouse directly, it creates an SPV.

The SPV becomes the legal owner of the property.

A licensed custodian stores all ownership documents and maintains secure records.

Blockchain records token ownership, while smart contracts distribute rental income according to the project's legal agreements.

Investors own tokens representing defined economic rights, while the SPV and custodian ensure the property is properly managed and protected.
