# Oracles, Custody and Off-Chain Data

Blockchain is excellent at storing and verifying digital information, but it cannot automatically know what happens in the real world. Real-World Asset (RWA) projects therefore depend on trusted mechanisms that connect blockchain with external information.

Three important components help achieve this:

- Oracles
- Custody
- Off-chain data

Together, they ensure that blockchain records remain connected to the actual asset they represent.

## 1. What Is an Oracle?

An oracle is a service that delivers verified information from outside the blockchain into a smart contract.

Because blockchains cannot access real-world information directly, oracles act as trusted data providers.

Examples of information an oracle may provide include:

- Asset prices.
- Exchange rates.
- Interest rates.
- Commodity prices.
- Weather information.
- Market indexes.
- Property valuation updates.

The oracle does not create the information—it simply delivers external data to the blockchain.

---

## 2. Why Oracles Matter

Many smart contracts depend on information from the real world.

For example:

- Rental income may change each month.
- Gold prices fluctuate daily.
- Interest rates change over time.
- Property valuations are periodically updated.

Without reliable external data, smart contracts cannot respond accurately to real-world events.

---

## 3. What Is Custody?

Custody refers to the safeguarding of assets or important legal documents.

Depending on the project, custody may involve:

- Physical assets.
- Ownership certificates.
- Precious metals.
- Legal agreements.
- Digital private keys.
- Financial records.

The custodian is responsible for protecting these assets according to agreed procedures.

---

## 4. Types of Custody

### Physical Custody

Used for assets such as:

- Gold.
- Silver.
- Artwork.
- Real estate documents.

---

### Digital Custody

Used for:

- Private keys.
- Digital wallets.
- Blockchain credentials.
- Token reserves.

Professional custodians often use advanced security procedures to reduce risk.

---

## 5. What Is Off-Chain Data?

Not every piece of information belongs on a blockchain.

Some information remains outside the blockchain because it may be:

- Confidential.
- Too large to store efficiently.
- Frequently updated.
- Protected by privacy laws.

Examples include:

- Property inspection reports.
- Insurance policies.
- Financial statements.
- Legal contracts.
- Customer identification documents.
- Maintenance records.

The blockchain may simply reference this information rather than storing it directly.

---

## 6. Working Together

A typical RWA project combines all three components.

For example:

**Blockchain records:**

- Token ownership.
- Transaction history.
- Smart contract execution.

**Oracle provides:**

- Updated market prices.
- Interest rates.
- External financial data.

**Custodian protects:**

- The underlying asset.
- Legal ownership documents.
- Sensitive records.

**Off-chain systems store:**

- Contracts.
- Compliance records.
- Valuation reports.
- Insurance documents.

Each component plays an important role in maintaining trust.

---

## 7. Risks to Consider

Projects should evaluate:

- Oracle reliability.
- Custodian reputation.
- Data accuracy.
- Cybersecurity.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Backup procedures.

Weakness in any one area may affect the overall integrity of the project.

---

## Practical Example

Imagine a company tokenizes a gold reserve.

The blockchain records token ownership.

A trusted oracle provides the daily market price of gold.

An independent custodian securely stores the physical gold.

Ownership certificates, insurance policies and audit reports remain stored off-chain but are referenced by the project.

Together, these systems help ensure that the digital tokens remain connected to the underlying asset.

## Key Takeaways

- Oracles connect blockchain with trusted external information.
- Custodians protect physical assets, digital assets or legal documentation.
- Off-chain data stores information that should not be placed directly on the blockchain.
- Successful RWA projects combine blockchain, oracles, custody and off-chain systems.
- Every component must be secure, reliable and properly managed.
